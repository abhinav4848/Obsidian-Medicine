# Manage patient coming with urinary symptoms
1. Get bloods done- FBC, U&e, LFT, HbA1c, Bone profile, [[prostate#PSA test (is linked)|PSA]]
	1. U&E helps see for obstructive uropathy if chronic retention suspected. 
2. get **urine dip** to rule out infection/blood. Referral for 2ww includes question on whether you tested for UTI. 
	1. if there's haematuria, then think where it could come from- [[cancer- urology|bladder]], kidney, possibly prostate. That's where smoking becomes a risk factor for kidney/bladder cancers. It's not a risk factor towards prostate cancer itself. 
3. DRE 
4. Risk factors for [[cancer- prostate|Prostate Cancer]]: Age > 50, [[afro-caribbean|Afro Caribbean]], [[cancer- risk factors|family history]].

# BPH
Patients have storage and voiding symptoms. 
- *See [[incontinence- adult]]*. 
- Can lead to UTI, Retention, obstructive uropathy
##### Alpha-1 blockers
Tamsulosin, doxazosin, alfuzosin
- Considered First line if IPSS >= 8 (mod-severe voiding symptoms)
- Alpha blockers are given to relax smooth muscles of bladder and prostate for fast relief (BPH can cause acute retention). And also if just LUT features. 
- They don't slow the growth of BPH. 
- **s/e**: First dose hypotension (may feel woozy, giddy), postural hypotension, Headache/dizziness, Dyspnoea, Cough

##### Anticholinergics %%(transcluded)%%
*See [[incontinence- adult|Urge incontinence]]*
When a patient with BPH exhibits persistent mixed urinary symptoms (storage & voiding) despite monotherapy with an alpha-blocker, can give antimuscarinic agent such as tolterodine, darifenacin, oxybutynin.

##### 5-alpha reductase inhibitor
Finasteride
- Given to those with high risk of progression of BPH. i.e. LUT + Large prostate (more than 30 gram) + raised PSA > 1.4
- Works by blocking Testosterone conversion to Dihydrotestosterone, which is known to induce BPH. 
- It reduces prostate volume & takes up-to 6 months to start acting. Can also reduce PSA. 
- **s/e**: Decreased libido, [[sildenafil|Erectile dysfunction]], Ejaculation problems, [[gynaecomastia|Gynecomastia]], breast tenderness, depression. 
	- *Finasteride is not a fun ride when you have erectile dysfunction* #mnemonic 
- Finasteride is also used in Androgenic [[alopecia]] in men. 

##### Combined Alpha blocker + 5-alpha reductase inhibitor
- Indicated by MTOPS trial- If the man has bothersome mod-severe voiding symptoms & prostatic enlargement, can use both together. 
##### Surgery
1. TURP
	- **s/e**: [[Hyponatremia]]: Confusion & Agitation. 
2. TUVP: Transurethral vaporization of prostate
3. Laser treatment of prostate
4. Prostatectomy

# Prostatitis
## Cause
- Infection or inflammation of prostate gland. 
- Can be post biopsy: E coli is common cause.
- UTI or STI could have backtracked to prostate. 
## Clinical Features
Perineal pain: hurts when sitting on hard chair. 
Cloudy urine and foul smell, abdominal pain.
Will be tender on DRE. 
Fever, tachycardia, can't take oral abx: Sepsis, retention - Need admission. 
## Treatment
**Investigation**: MUS, Bloods & culture, Consider STI screen

If patient normal: E coli
	**Rx**: [[fluroquinolone|Quinolone]] ([[ciprofloxacin]], ofloxacin) > Co-trimoxazole #abx 
	14 day course of Quinolone for Prostatitis: Cipro, Ofloxacin empirically
	Quinolones penetrate the prostate better than other antibiotics. 
If with calculus
	**Rx**: TURP 
If immunocompromised: Pseudomonas

Review in 48 hrs -> If not improving, for admission. 
There's also chronic prostatitis (> 3 months) needing abx for 4-6 weeks. 
- Urology guidance based. 
- Discuss pain relief with patient.

# PSA test %%(is linked)%%
See: *[[cancer- screening programs]] & [[tumour marker]]* & [CKS: PSA test](https://cks.nice.org.uk/topics/prostate-cancer/diagnosis/assessment/#psa-testing) 

> [!notice]- Doctor can I have a PSA test?
> - What do you know about PSA tests
> - Did anything trigger the request?
> - Are you aware of what happens if it is raised?
> - What do you know about prostate cancer?

Not a sensitive test. 
- **False Positive**: 3/4th of raised PSAs can be normal (i.e. have a negative biopsy). So can lead to unnecessary investigation and treatment. 
- **False Negative**: Around 15% of negative tests will actually have cancer. 
Do consider this test in Men > 50yo with [[incontinence- adult|LUTS]], [[erectile dysfunction|ED]], visible [[haematuria]]. 

NICE says '*If a hard, irregular prostate typical of a prostate carcinoma is felt on rectal examination, then the patient should be referred urgently. The PSA should be measured and the result should accompany the referral.*' 

However in an asymptomatic patient where you can't do PSA yet, a digital rectal examination is not a substitute for a PSA test. 

Don’t have before test:
- 48 hrs.: Ejaculation
- 48 hrs.: Exercised vigorously
- 1 wk.: Digital rectal exam
- 4 wk.: UTI (PSA is always high during & after UTI), Prostatitis
- 6 wk.: prostate biopsy
	
PSA levels rise naturally with age, so thresholds change: 
- Age < 40 or >80yo: Use clinical judgement
- 40-49: >=2.5
- 50-59: >=3.5
- 60-69: >= 4.5
- 70-79: >= 6.5
- > 79: >20 or >7.5 + symptoms suggestive of metastatic disease
	- In those men above the age of 79 if the initial PSA test is 7.5–20 ng/mL and there are no symptoms suggestive of metastatic disease repeat the PSA once after 6 months.
	- After repeating PSA refer via a suspected cancer pathway if any of the following occur:
	    - There are symptoms suggestive of metastatic disease.
	    - The PSA level has risen above 20.
	    - The PSA has more than doubled and the patient has a performance status of 0 or 1.
Do a 2ww referral. *See [[cancer- prostate|Prostate Cancer]].*
