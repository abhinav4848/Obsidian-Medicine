# Types
## Erythroplasia of Queyrat
An in-situ squamous cell carcinoma. #cancer ([PCDS](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/genital-conditions-an-overview) & [[cancer- penis]]). It presents with single or multiple plaques with a red, velvety appearance and is often asymptomatic. 

[[lichens|Lichen Sclerosis]] can be mistaken for EoQ. The 'glistening' erythema can have a similar appearance to EoQ. 
## Bowenoid papulosis (BP)
Associated with [[viral wart|HPV]]. 
- Occurs in younger sexually active men as opposed to the lesions of QOQ and Bowen's disease which are usually in older men. 
- Lesions tend to be less papillomatous, flatter-topped, more polymorphic, and more coalescent than common genital viral warts ([[human papilloma virus|Condyloma acuminatum]])
## [[cancer- skin|bowen's disease]] of penis
- Red, or sometimes hyperpigmented, scaly patches or plaques of the keratinised penis. Lesions can occasionally have a warty or crusty appearance.
## SCC Penis
Ulcer with raised edge.
Prev h/o PeIN, [[lichens|Lichen Sclerosis]]. 
# Investigation
- Both erythroplasia of Queyrat and Bowen's disease require urgent biopsy to exclude invasive squamous cell carcinoma. 
- A biopsy is also needed to see if the changes are undifferentiated (HPV-related), or differentiated (lichen sclerosus-related)
