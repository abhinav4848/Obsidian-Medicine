The average woman in the UK goes through the menopause when she is 51 years old. 
Menopause is always 12 months of amenorrhoea.
### Menopause & Contraception / HRT %%embedded%%
The rule for duration of use is different for contraception & HRT.  
[[contraceptives|Contraception]] :  
- If age < 50yo: Use contraception for 24 months since LMP 
- If age > 50yo: Only 12 months since LMP 
  
[[hrt|HRT]]:  
- Cyclical / Sequential HRT is < 12 months since LMP (perimenopause)  
- Continuous HRT is > 12 months since LMP (Menopause)
# Effects of Menopause
> [!NOTE]- Symptoms of Menopause: Image
> ![Menopause symptoms|400](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/d9/Symptoms_of_menopause_%28raster%29.png)

- Menopausal symptoms (can last for 5-7 years)
	- Vasomotor symptoms e.g. hot flushes, night sweats. (called 'Typical Symptoms'). 
	- Menstrual irregularities. 
	- Sleep disturbance (due to night sweats or low mood)
	- Urinary problems.
	- Joint and muscle pains.
	- Mood changes (anxiety, loss of confidence, mood swings, irritability)
	- Cognitive disturbance.
	- Headache & Fatigue
	- Urogenital Atrophy
		- Vaginal dryness.
		- Bladder symptoms: Urgency, Stress incontinence, Recurrent [[uti|UTI]]. 
	- Loss of libido: Could be due to hormone deficiency +- dyspareunia due to urogenital atrophy. 
- Osteoporosis
- Loss of muscle mass & strength

> [!tip]- HRT / Menopause Assessment- Information to Clarify
> - Any other symptoms and their **severity and impact** on her life at home and at work.
> - **Bleeding pattern** and **LMP** if she has not had a hysterectomy.
> - History of **cervical and breast [[cancer- screening programs|Screening]]**.
> - Does she need/use **contraception**?
> - Other medical history and regular medication, including:
>   - obesity
>   - gynaecological malignancies or surgery, particularly whether she has had a **hysterectomy**
>   - cardiovascular disease or risk factors
>   - diabetes
>   - personal or family history of **venous thromboembolism (VTE)**
>   - migraine
>   - anything prescribed elsewhere or bought over the counter from a pharmacist, health food shop or herbalist. This might include:
>     - herbal preparations bought specifically for the menopause
>     - products commonly bought to aid symptoms such as sleep and stress, such as **CBD preparations**
> - **Social history**, including: work pattern, smoking, alcohol, exercise, diet, stress levels
> - Family history of **breast, endometrial or ovarian cancer**.
>
> **Examination**
> - **Genital examination is not routinely needed**, but may be appropriate if the history identifies concerning symptoms such as an **abnormal bleeding pattern**.
> - She should be booked for her **cervical screening test** if it is overdue.
> - ***Blood pressure and BMI should be documented before starting HRT.***
> 	- Obesity with BMI > 30 means prefer transdermal rather than oral HRT.
> 	- Uncontrolled HTN can cause CVD/CAD. Untreated HTN should be controlled before starting HRT. 

# FSH
Premature Menopause: <40yo 
Early menopause: <45yo
- 2 results of FSH >30 IU/L 4-8 weeks apart
Perimenopause
- In women >= 45yo, perimenopause can be identified without lab tests. 
- FSH levels can fluctuate during perimenopause & normal FSH doesn't rule out perimenopause.

# Treatment options
- Lifestyle, 
- HRT: First line for vasomotor symptoms, low mood. 
- Alternatives to HRT: CBT for mood/anxiety, soy, SSRI, SNRI (Don't use fluoxetine or paroxetine for patients taking tamoxifen), Clonidine

# Reviews for patients on HRT %%linked%%
3 months after starting then at least annually
- discuss symptom control, s/e, ongoing indication
- check BP, BMI. 
### Stopping HRT
There's no end date for stopping HRT. Withdraw when risks outweigh benefits & patient happy to stop. 
Consider gradual withdrawal if high doses. 

If HRT was started for premature menopause
- should be continued until the age of 50 years
- the most important reason in giving HRT to younger women is preventing the development of osteoporosis
