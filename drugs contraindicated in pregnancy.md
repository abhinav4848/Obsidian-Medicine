## Antibiotics — contraindications and cautions in pregnancy
Restrictions depend on the drug, trimester and indication; some antibiotics may still be used when clinically necessary.

| Antibiotic / group | Pregnancy concern and use |
|---|---|
| **Tetracyclines**, including doxycycline | Generally avoid, particularly after about **13 weeks**, because of **tooth discolouration** and potential effects on developing bone. Early exposure is not known to increase the risk of birth defects; use may occasionally be justified when no suitable alternative exists. [BUMPS](https://www.medicinesinpregnancy.org/leaflets-a-z/tetracycline/), [NHS antimicrobial guidance](https://www.nottsapc.nhs.uk/media/ho3fopw1/pregnancy_and_breastfeeding.pdf). |
| **Aminoglycosides** | **Fetal ototoxicity** and potential nephrotoxicity. **[[tuberculosis\|Streptomycin]]** is particularly associated with fetal hearing damage. Reserve systemic use for situations where benefits outweigh risks and suitable alternatives are unavailable; **gentamicin** is preferred when an aminoglycoside is necessary. Topical eye/ear preparations are a separate consideration. [UKTIS](https://uktis.org/monographs/use-of-aminoglycoside-antibiotics-in-pregnancy/). |
| **Sulphonamides and [[Trimethoprim]]** | **Trimethoprim:** [[folic acid]] antagonist; avoid especially in **T1** because of possible neural-tube and other defects. If essential in early pregnancy, give **folic acid 5 mg daily**.<br>**Sulphonamides**, e.g. sulfamethoxazole: concern near delivery about neonatal **hyperbilirubinaemia and haemolysis**, particularly with prematurity or G6PD deficiency; avoid near term where possible.<br>**Co-trimoxazole = trimethoprim + sulfamethoxazole.** [UKTIS](https://uktis.org/monographs/use-of-trimethoprim-in-pregnancy/). |
| **Quinolones**, e.g. [[Ciprofloxacin]] | **BNF advises avoidance because arthropathy has occurred in animal studies.** Prefer a suitable alternative; a quinolone may be used when necessary for a serious infection if alternatives are unsuitable. Human pregnancy data do not establish the same fetal joint toxicity. [BNF](https://bnf.nice.org.uk/treatment-summaries/quinolones/), [BUMPS](https://www.medicinesinpregnancy.org/leaflets-a-z/quinolone-antibiotics/). |
| **Chloramphenicol** | Systemic use is reserved for serious infections; near-term use raises concern about **grey baby syndrome** #named. Eye/ear preparations are not subject to the same blanket restriction. [UKTIS](https://uktis.org/monographs/use-of-chloramphenicol-in-pregnancy/). |
| **[[Nitrofurantoin]]** | A first-choice option for **lower [[UTI]]** in pregnancy when suitable. **Avoid at term and during labour/delivery** because of neonatal **haemolysis** risk; later-T3 use requires a benefit–risk assessment. [NICE NG109](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng109/chapter/recommendations), [UKTIS](https://uktis.org/monographs/use-of-nitrofurantoin-in-pregnancy/). |
| **[[Macrolides]]** | Not a blanket class contraindication. **Erythromycin** has historically been preferred because of greater pregnancy experience; other macrolides may be used when clinically appropriate. [UKTIS](https://uktis.org/monographs/use-of-clarithromycin-in-pregnancy/). |
**Related doxycycline notes:** [[chlamydia]], [[lyme disease]] and leptospirosis; choose a pregnancy-specific regimen for the indication. For paediatric use, see [BNFc doxycycline](https://bnfc.nice.org.uk/drugs/doxycycline/).

**Sulfasalazine (RA/IBD):** can be used in pregnancy on specialist advice; give high-dose folic acid. [BUMPS](https://www.medicinesinpregnancy.org/leaflets-a-z/sulfasalazine/).

UTI Alternatives:
- Amoxicillin (but only if culture shows sensitivity),
- Cefalexin (1st gen cephalosporin. 2nd choice in UTI after Nitrofurantoin. Even if culture not available. Plus it can be used for both [[pyelonephritis|upper]] and lower UTI. Has 10% cross reactivity with penicillin but this is too low to worry about),
- Erythromycin

# Pregnancy Prevention program with selected drugs
Following meds are required to have pregnancy prevention programme. Following medications require it:  
- valproate  
- topiramate  
- thalidomide  
- lenalidomide  
- pomalidomide  
- vismodegib  
- isotretinoin  
- acitretin  
- alitretinoin
