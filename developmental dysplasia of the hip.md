The head of femur can easily come out of the acetabulum -> Unstable joint -> Subluxation/ Dislocation. 
Doesn't cause pain in the child but can lead to early hip replacement. 
# Risk factors
First fat female born oligo breech in a family with such history. 
	Firstborn, birth wt. > 5kgs, Girl child, oligohydramnios, breech presentation, family history,
Age < 3yo
*All breech presentations noted at 36 completed weeks of gestation require USS for DDH [[newborn Infant physical examination|screening]] regardless of*
- mode of delivery at birth (Normal or C-section).
- presentation at birth
# Clinical Features
[[limping child|Limping]].
Painless leg that is shorter than the other. 
Unequal skin folds (not a criteria for NIPE infant exam anymore) 
### Screening tests 
| **Manoeuvre**            | **Purpose**                                                          | **How to perform**                                                                                                                                                                   | **Positive finding**                                                           | **Key point / mnemonic**                                                                    |
| ------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Barlow's manoeuvre**   | Tests hip instability by attempting to dislocate an unstable hip | Examine one hip at a time, stabilising the contralateral hip. Flex and adduct hip, then apply gentle downward/posterior pressure through the knee                            | Femoral head dislocates posteriorly. Felt as a clunk/pop               | Barlow = dislocate → attempts to push an articulated femoral head out of the acetabulum |
| **Ortolani's manoeuvre** | Tests for and reduces a dislocated hip                           | Flex hip and knee; place fingers over the greater trochanter, thumb near groin crease. Gently abduct the thigh while applying upward/anterior pressure to the trochanter | Femoral head relocates into the acetabulum, producing a palpable clunk | Ortolani = relocate → brings a dislocated femoral head back into the acetabulum         |
Note: Barlow might be negative if hip is already dislocated at the time of presentation. 

Galleazi #named:In infants > 3 mo, assessment by the Barlow and Ortolani tests is no longer sensitive. Perform the Galleazi sign to test for loss of abduction of the affected hip. 
# Investigation
- Confirm with USS as although can be self-resolving, it presents as a spectrum, so can be bad as well. 
- USS from 6 weeks up to 4.5 months cuz hips are cartilaginous. (see [[newborn Infant physical examination|Newborn & Infant Physical Examination]])
- Xray only after 4.5 months. 
# Management 
Most unstable hips will spontaneously stabilize by 3-6 weeks. 
At [[newborn Infant physical examination|6-8 week check]], if barlow/ortolani is positive, urgent refer to paeds ortho. 

Pavlik harness #named(dynamic flexion abduction orthosis) in children <4-6 months

Other children: Surgery.
