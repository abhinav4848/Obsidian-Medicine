Male sterilisation - vasectomy  
- **What happens**: Occluding the vas deferens so there's no sperm in the ejaculate
- **Can be achieved by**: Cauterisation, ligation, clips, intra-vas devices. 
	- simple operation, can be done under LA (some GA), go home after a couple of hours
- **Ask the trigger for wanting this**: *Where has this come from? Is this something they've thought about for a while? Something they thought about last night? In a relationship? Do they have children? Want children in the future?* #sca Reversal is not easy and not offered on NHS. 
	- **Reversal**: the success rate of vasectomy reversal is up to 55%, if done within 10 years, and approximately 25% after more than 10 years.
- **Doesn't work immediately**: Semen analysis needs to be performed twice following a vasectomy before a man can have unprotected sex (usually at 12 weeks).
- **Failure rate**: 1 per 2,000 (0.05%) - male sterilisation is a more effective method of contraception than female sterilisation. 	*Female sterilisation failure rate of 1:200*
- **Complications**: bruising, haematoma, infection, sperm granuloma, chronic testicular pain/post vasectomy pain (affects between 5-30% men)

---

*The semen does not get trapped in the scrotum after a vasectomy. In this procedure, the vas deferens are cut or blocked, which prevents sperm from reaching the seminal fluid (semen) that is ejaculated from the penis. The sperm cells are still produced by the testicles but they are reabsorbed back into the body. Therefore, there is no accumulation of sperm within the scrotum post-vasectomy.*
