Pox virus.
peak incidence 1-4yo. 
White/Pink papules with an umblicated (depressed) central punctum.
If squeezed, they produce cheesy/white material.
# Clinical Features
Spreads by direct contacts, swimming, fomites -> avoid bath/sharing with siblings.
Affects children, and immunocompromised (AIDS) rather than elderly.
Conditions like [[eczema]] lower the skin barrier and increase chance of infection.
# Treatment
- Reassure.
- Resolves spontaneously in 6-24 months. 
- No need to exclude from [[school in children with rash|school]]/gym/pool.
- Explain that lesions are contagious, and it is sensible to avoid sharing towels, clothing, and baths with uninfected people (e.g. siblings). 
- Advise not to scratch/pick/squeeze lesions as this can cause auto-innoculation to other areas + superadded infection. 

**Rx**: Although therapeutic options such as podophyllotoxin, imiquimod, or cryotherapy are available, there is limited evidence to support their effectiveness. These treatments act by stimulating an immune response to clear the virus rather than targeting the virus directly. 
# Referrals
- If eyelid involved & [[red eye]] -> specialist referral.
- If [[HIV]] +ve with extensive lesion -> HIV specialist
- If Anogenital lesion -> refer to GUM for screening for other STI.
