# SCA mock exam framework
## What the official assessment measures
The three domains are **Data gathering and diagnosis**, **Clinical management and medical complexity**, and **Relating to others**. Domain grades are CP, P, F and CF, judged against the standard of a newly qualified independent GP. Management carries additional weighting. Examiners apply descriptors to a case-specific schedule; these are not universal tick boxes. Overall results aggregate performance across the examination, with case standards set using borderline regression. A fixed checklist percentage cannot reproduce this. [RCGP marking guidance](https://www.rcgp.org.uk/mrcgp-exams/simulated-consultation-assessment/marking-and-results)

The examination has twelve 12-minute consultations, each preceded by three minutes of reading. It includes video and audio consultations, sometimes with a parent/carer or professional. Physical examination performance is assessed through WPBA rather than the SCA. There is no prescribed consultation script. [RCGP format](https://www.rcgp.org.uk/mrcgp-exams/simulated-consultation-assessment/introduction)

## How to use this framework
These sections provide a paraphrased working map of the published descriptor areas for future consultations and mock SCA cases. They are prompts for reviewing relevant performance, not 46 compulsory tasks in every consultation. Apply each area only when the scenario warrants it. The linked RCGP page remains the reference for the exact wording.

## Domain 1 — Data gathering and diagnosis
- DG1: Focus questions on the presenting problem and safety.
- DG2: Use existing records and contextual information.
- DG3: Identify relevant warning features and pertinent negatives.
- DG4: Explore psychological and social context.
- DG5: Develop a reasoned, evidence-informed differential.
- DG6: Weigh likelihood using epidemiology and illness trajectory.
- DG7: Update hypotheses when evidence changes.
- DG8: Integrate incomplete information into a defensible working assessment.

Sources: [RCGP descriptors](https://www.rcgp.org.uk/mrcgp-exams/simulated-consultation-assessment/marking-and-results), [clinical knowledge and diagnostic capability](https://www.rcgp.org.uk/mrcgp-exams/gp-curriculum/applying-clinical-knowledge-and-skill).

## Domain 2 — Clinical management and medical complexity
Clinical management:
- CM1: Use observation when clinically suitable.
- CM2: Support understanding and practical self-care.
- CM3: Apply relevant treatment guidance.
- CM4: Prescribe safely.
- CM5: Refer appropriately; consider resources.
- CM6: Arrange proportionate review and continuity.
- CM7: Coordinate care across teams/services.
- CM8: Adapt options to circumstances and preferences.
- CM9: Justify difficult management decisions safely.
- CM10: Find workable, flexible solutions.

Complexity and holistic care:
- CM11: Address interacting acute and chronic conditions.
- CM12: Prioritise according to risk.
- CM13: Manage clinical and patient uncertainty.
- CM14: Adjust care for multimorbidity and medication burden.
- CM15: Consider prevention, recovery and health improvement.
- CM16: Involve appropriate family/carer support services.
- CM17: Act on safeguarding concerns, including necessary sharing/referral.

Sources: [RCGP descriptors](https://www.rcgp.org.uk/mrcgp-exams/simulated-consultation-assessment/marking-and-results), [clinical management capability](https://www.rcgp.org.uk/mrcgp-exams/gp-curriculum/applying-clinical-knowledge-and-skill), [complexity and coordination](https://www.rcgp.org.uk/mrcgp-exams/gp-curriculum/managing-complex-and-long-term-care).

## Domain 3 — Relating to others
Professional and ethical practice:
- R1: Respect patients without discrimination.
- R2: Own decisions; recognise personal limits.
- R3: Respect cultural and personal differences.
- R4: Recognise individual values and beliefs.
- R5: Respond fairly without judgement.
- R6: Respect patient choice.
- R7: Act for the patient's benefit.
- R8: Address consent, capacity and best-interests issues appropriately.

Communication:
- R9: Explore the person's agenda, beliefs and preferences.
- R10: Listen actively; communicate verbally and nonverbally.
- R11: Notice cues; use accessible language.
- R12: Adapt explanation methods and supporting materials.
- R13: Tailor explanations to this person's understanding and priorities.
- R14: Negotiate an understandable, acceptable plan.
- R15: Check understanding.
- R16: Show empathy and willingness to help.

Colleagues and wider context:
- R17: Collaborate with colleagues and value their contributions.
- R18: Treat colleagues fairly and respectfully.
- R19: Recognise effects on the patient, family and carers.
- R20: Challenge harmful beliefs/behaviours respectfully.
- R21: Work together around what matters to the patient.

Sources: [RCGP descriptors](https://www.rcgp.org.uk/mrcgp-exams/simulated-consultation-assessment/marking-and-results), [ethical and communication capabilities](https://www.rcgp.org.uk/mrcgp-exams/gp-curriculum/knowing-yourself-and-relating-to-others).

## Instructions for Codex when I request a mock SCA case
These are our custom practice rules, not claims about official examiner procedures.

1. Read this note when I request a custom SCA mock. Default to one case; do not reveal the diagnosis or marking guide beforehand.
2. Prepare a consistent candidate brief, private role-player brief and private assessment guide before starting. Specify consultation medium, who is present, relevant records and any supplied findings.
3. Make the scenario feasible within 12 minutes. Include realistic uncertainty, patient priorities and a limited number of meaningful challenges. Do not cram every descriptor into one case.
4. During role-play, respond as the specified patient, parent or professional. Reveal information naturally in response to appropriate questions and cues. Do not coach or give marks until the case ends unless I request teaching mode.
5. Keep facts stable. Never invent a reassuring examination or test result to justify a decision. If information is unavailable, say so. Acknowledge appropriate plans to obtain necessary assessment without requiring an imaginary physical examination performance.
6. Use current UK primary guidance to prepare and check consequential clinical decisions. Verify doses against an appropriate current prescribing source before making them part of the assessment. State unresolved uncertainty.
7. Accept different safe approaches and natural wording. Do not require a memorised phrase, exhaustive differential, irrelevant investigation or generic lifestyle speech.
8. Adapt expectations to the consultation medium, participants, communication needs and available information. Evaluate how the clinician recognises and handles relevant limitations.
9. If timing tools are available, measure elapsed time. Otherwise state that timing is approximate; do not invent time stamps.

Across multiple sessions, vary the case mix. The RCGP blueprint includes children; reproductive/sexual health; long-term illness; older adults/end-of-life care; mental health/addiction; urgent care; disadvantage/vulnerability; diversity; undifferentiated presentations; prescribing; investigations/results; and professional dilemmas. These can overlap within cases. [RCGP preparation and blueprint](https://www.rcgp.org.uk/mrcgp-exams/simulated-consultation-assessment/preparing)

## Feedback format after each mock
Use the following educational format:

| Domain | Provisional CP / P / F / CF | Evidence from the consultation | Important gap and why it mattered |
|---|---|---|---|
| Data gathering and diagnosis | | | |
| Clinical management and medical complexity | | | |
| Relating to others | | | |

- Quote or accurately paraphrase what I actually said. Distinguish observed behaviour from an inference.
- For relevant learning points, use **demonstrated / partly demonstrated / not demonstrated / not applicable / insufficient evidence**. Missing transcript content is not automatically proof that an action never happened.
- Judge each domain as a whole. Do not convert the descriptor list into an equally weighted score, invent an official pass mark, or predict my examination result.
- Identify any important safety problem separately, explaining the risk and safer alternative. Do not claim an automatic examination fail without a verified rule.
- Give three prioritised improvements and one brief example of better wording. Offer a focused retry of the weakest part.
- Distinguish necessary actions from optional refinements. Avoid penalising the same omission repeatedly without explaining its different effects.

Official feedback is selected after marking; it is not itself a scoring checklist. RCGP educational guidance emphasises relevant investigations, proportionate follow-up, selective questioning, time management and responsive communication. Our feedback should reflect those principles. [RCGP feedback guidance](https://www.rcgp.org.uk/mrcgp-exams/simulated-consultation-assessment/feedback-statements)
