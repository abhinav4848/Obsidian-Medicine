Must do bits: Red flag bubble, Social History (PSY Bubble), ICE bubble

# Introduction
Early open questions: 
- Weak: Tell me more about... 
- Strong: *You mentioned the pain, tell me more about the pain. Oh you mentioned work problems, tell me more about that.* 

Pain: SOCRATES
- Can you point to where the pain is?
- onset: how did it start? gradually?
- character: can you describe it? crushing/tight...
- radiation: Does it move? 
- association? (Anything else with it?): e.g headache: Is there anything else happening alongside it? What about change in your vision? Any funny sensation in your fingers? Any funny sounds in your ear? 
- Timing: we know how it started but how's it been since?
- exacerbating/relieving. 
- Severity. 

Others/Cough/palpitation: ODIPARA

# Past Medical History
Have you been diagnosed with any conditions? 
Do you have any known medical problems?
Have you been admitted to hospital?
Have you had any operations? 

h/o Asthma: have you been admitted to hospital before? Have you ever been given oral steroids? 

Don't do "MCJThreads": MI, Cancer, Jaundice, HTN, Rheumatic fever, epilepsy, asthma, diabetes, stroke.
# Drug History
Mr. x, looking at the notes, I can see from my notes that you take so and so meds... Is that right? 
Are you prescribed any regular medications? 
Do you always take these? (compliance issues)

including: 
- those prescribed (who prescribed and how long are they prescribed for)
- bought OTC
- injections (they're also meds)- insulin, injection for prostate, contraceptives as medication
- contraception (female with migraines: Can I just check if you take any contraceptive tablets or pills)
- herbal and other alternative therapy drugs? 

### Allergies
Do you have any known allergies?
if they do say they have allergy to something: What happens when you take these? (Why did this term come about?- tummy irritation vs full blown anaphylaxis)

Mr. X, when it comes to indigestion, we do prescribe some medications for this, just checking... Have you got any allergies that you know of? 

# Family History

# Psychosocial
Ask based on relevance. Ask in a two way approach: How is life impacting medicine and medicine impacting life. 
### Home: 
- Who else lives at home- **Support systems**
	- Are they wife / kids?
		- What does your wife think about this? Have you spoken to her about this? 
			- You can later on bring it back like *You mentioned your wife thinks this*, *You mentioned your wife already helps you with this*
	- Are you a carer for somebody. 
	- If I am to break some bad news to you right now, who have you got to discuss with when you're stressed/worried. 
- **ADLs**- Shopping/Cooking/Cleaning
	- How are you getting on with these. 
	- Do you have any carers/family
### Work:
- Certain environments can trigger a cough, like cold environments, dusty environments. Can I ask what you do for a job? 
	- has your job been impacted by this? Has there been an environment change recently? Have you taken any time off? Are you employed / self employed? 
	- If you are going to tell somebody to take time off, knowing this is useful. *You mentioned you haven't told your boss about this, do you think this is a good thing to do?*
- When it comes to headaches, there are some links to it in our life, can I ask a few things about your lifestyle. 
### Straight problem: Smoking/Alcohol causing the problem
**Smoking**:
- You mentioned this cough, one thing that can make this cough worse is smoking. Can I check if you smoke? 

**Alcohol**: 
- You mentioned some indigestion. Things like Alcohol can do it. Can I check if you drink any alcohol?

**Other problems causing use of smoking/alcohol.**

You mentioned things have been difficult recently. Sometimes when people go through difficult times, they may turn to things they think may help them cope. 
- Things like smoking, things like alcohol. Can I check if you do any of those? 

Sometimes when people are in pain for long time, they can turn to other things which they think may help them cope....
- Things like smoking, things like alcohol even. Can I check if you do any of those?

# ICE
## Ideas
Don't ask very narrow questions like... You have back pain. What do you think it could be?

Instead ask about underlying health beliefs:
- What were your thought about these symptoms?
- Was anything going through your mind about all of this? 
- What were your own thoughts on this?
- How much are you aware about XXX?

Even for a condition which is already known to the patient, say Migraine and they've come to ask for prophylaxis: 
- How much are you aware about prophylaxis
- How much are you aware about migraine?
## Concerns
**Not**: What were you worried this could be? (Only talks about the condition)
**Instead ask**: Was there anything worrying you about all of this? (Worried it could be cancer/worried if I can do my trip in a month)
## Expectations
- Was there anything in particular that you wanted from this consultation?
- Did you have anything in mind as to how we could help here?
- When you booked the appointment today, was there anything that you specifically were looking for? 

## Reflection on the ICE
### Example 1:  Low Back Pain
**Idea**: So mister X, you've had this back pain now for a while, it seems like. What were your thoughts about all of this? What was going through your mind? 
- *Patient*: I don't know, doctor. I do a lot of lifting at work and probably pulled a muscle, to be honest.
- *Reply*: Fair enough. You know what... we see Muscle pulls of backs all the time is really common, and I can see why you think that. But, obviously, this is affecting you to the point that You've come to see us about this. What was particularly worrying you about all of this?

**Concern**: 
- *Patient*: Yeah. I do a lot of lifting at work, doc. And what if I can't lift? I don't wanna lose my job. I'm really stressed about losing my job. 
- *Reply*: You know what? Fair enough. That sounds very reasonable. Obviously, work is important. We'll talk about your work in a bit of detail. Maybe there's ways that we can help. Promise we'll talk about that. Now, obviously, you've come here. You've tried medication. You've tried some stretching and things. Was there anything particular that you thought we could do when you came here today? 

**Expectation**: 
- *Patient*: Well, my uncle, had some back problems, and he had an MRI scan, and it showed something. I don't know what it was, but I'm just wondering whether I need a scan just to make sure everything's okay.
- *Reply*: You know, fair enough. We do do scans for back sometimes, not in every case, but maybe once you've had a bit of a chat and maybe seen you at some point, can make a decision as to whether a scan is right for you or not.

Just reflecting that, yeah, I get why that comes from. I can see where that comes from. Yeah. That makes sense now. Maybe we can help with that. That's a really good thing to talk about. We'll talk about that later.  
### Example 2: Depression
**Idea**: Now, Mr x, you've obviously felt low for a while now. What what were your thoughts about all of this? Why do you think this is all happening? 
- *Patient*: I don't know, doctor. I've I've just got too much stress. Like I said, work's crazy, and I think it's just the work getting on top of me, to be honest. I think I'm just getting too stressed.
- *Reply*: Fair enough. Stress is a big thing, an important thing, and maybe that's linked. And we will talk about your work and how we might be able to help you with that. But, obviously, you've come to see us about this. Was there anything particularly worrying you about all of this?

**Concern**: 
- *Patient*: Well, I'm in a relationship, and I'm noticing it having an impact on that. And I really don't want my relation to end, and that's why I thought it's really important to come at this point. 
- *Reply*: Fair enough. I think that's an important thing that you've mentioned, and we'll talk about your relationship. Maybe there are things that we can do from that point of view as well, but I see where the worry comes from, and I promise you we'll look into that. Now, obviously, you've you've tried a few kind of self-help things online. Was there anything that maybe you thought we could do for all of this low mood when you came in?

**Expectation**: 
- *Patient*:  I don't know if I need to at this point or whether this usually happens, but maybe a psychiatrist. I don't know. Do I need to see one of those? I don't know if they're gonna be of any use. 
- *Reply*: You know, you're right. Sometimes we do refer people with low mood to psychiatry, but there's a lot of things that we could often do here as well. So maybe let's kind of finish off asking you a few questions and then figure out what we can do best. 

### Mixed Examples
You can end up with any bubble as the starting point. 

If someone comes in and says, doctor, I'm losing weight. I'm really struggling. 
- That's a red flag. You're probably gonna go more into the clinical side. *When people lose weight, there are a couple of yes, no questions we'd like to ask just to make sure things are okay.* So you're naturally into **red flag bubble**. 

Someone else might come in and say, Doctor, you know what? I'm really struggling with work. I just need to get on a call to somebody. I don't know what's going on. I need to talk to someone about it. 
- That's the **psychosocial** bubble. You're into that already. *Tell me about work. What's been going on? Have you spoken to your boss? Have you taken any time off? You're obviously stressed at work. What about at home? Who's supporting you at home? Who's at home? When people are stressed, they can turn to things to help cope.* Can you see how when you're in that bubble, you stay in that bubble and you finish that bubble? 

Someone might come in and say, doctor, I'm just I'm just really worried about cancer. 
- Now they've already started to open about their mind (**ICE**). *Tell me your thoughts. What's going on in your mind? Why are we thinking like this? Okay. You mentioned cancer. What particularly is worrying about all of this?*
## Common FAQs about ICE
- Can do ICE at any point. 
- Should I ask all the questions every time? Yes. Ask all the 3 questions. Even if they say No. 
- What if someone has already told me an answer? I know you mentioned you wanted a CT scan, but while you are here, you do look particularly affected by this. Is there anything else that was worrying you? 
- What if I get 3 No's? That's fine. 

# Examination
I noticed from a letter that it showed that you had this particular finding. There's no point reading this stuff and not showing that I've gathered the data. Your job is to show somebody watching or listening that I have gathered this data appropriately, and I'm using it as part of my management plan.

Sometimes when you talk about a management plan, you're going to be saying things like, *I think we need to get you in at some point to do an examination. What I'd like to do is this, this, and this*. 

Use examination findings historically. The data you read/gather from the notes will have use. Show that you've read it. e.g. Specialist letter, practice nurse data from yesterday... With the given data... you might get things to bring on: 
Mr X, I know that you came in yesterday. I know that your blood pressure was normal. I know that your heart rate was fine, but I noticed that your BMI was slightly higher than it was a few years ago.
# Investigation
There might be historical findings on notes. 
> Mr x, I noticed one year ago that your liver function test blood results were normal. It'd be interesting to see what they show now. 

Investigation findings that are recent. You might see a week ago, someone dropped in a urine sample and an MSU result came back negative. Again, gathering the data and letting it influence your management plan.
> Now Mr X, thank you firstly for sending that urine sample in last week. The results have come back normal, so that does change a little bit about what we think about going forward. 

There might be an MRI knee for example, there might be a CT head result. Again, you're gathering that data and using as part of your overall assessment terms of taking your management plan forward. Might even have a letter from a clinic and again there might be some investigation findings there as well. e.g. Mr. Y's biopsy showed a likely malignancy. This is obviously setting up for a breaking bad news case.
### When should we give the results? 
Don't give results early if they're not pressurising. 
When we give results early, patients are going to have questions and you haven't done your full data gathering yet. So you don't really know how to answer those questions properly and that can be quite problematic. 

Verbalize that you've got results early on. E.g. Someone's blood tests have come back, and your job is now to take this story forward. Acknowledge that you have the blood test. 
*Mr X, I can see that you came in last week. I can see that you got some blood tests done. I do have the results here, and we can go through those. But do mind if I ask you a couple of questions first just to understand the story and make sure we're on the right track?* Most of the times, if you're open about the fact that you've got results, but you'd like to ask a few questions first, most people are like, yeah. That's fine, doctor.

Then once you do your data gathering, that gives you a much better baseline to be able to give those results in the second half and talk about the implications. 
Of course, there are gonna be some cases where they're pushing for the results. "Yeah, doctor. I've I've been through all these questions. Just what did the results show? I'm really stressed. Or doctor, come on. Why are taking so long? Just tell me what did the X-ray showed. Then you have to adapt, of course. That's just being able to be flexible. *Look, Mr X, I can see that you're really keen to get these results. Normally, we ask a few questions first, but I can see you're really worried. Let's talk about the results now.* 

But just an important point, when you do get investigations given to you, don't feel like you need to give them at the beginning, but you do need to verbalize that you've got them, and then they'll come out in the second half.
