## Renal result or urine abnormality
Read the trend, creatinine/eGFR, potassium and urine findings with symptoms and timing. Ask output, haematuria, infection, colic/obstruction, fluid losses/intake, swelling/dyspnoea, drugs and baseline disease. Review NSAIDs, ACE inhibitor/ARB/diuretics, lithium, supplements and recent illness; do not assume every fall in eGFR is stable CKD.

Urine dip can show blood/protein/glucose/ketones but does not answer every question. Quantify albumin with ACR; dipstick can miss clinically relevant albuminuria. Interpret glucose with diabetes/SGLT2 treatment and ketones with illness/starvation/pregnancy context.
## AKI
Consider pre-renal hypoperfusion, intrinsic disease and obstruction. Adult criteria include creatinine rise ≥26 micromol/L within 48 hours, ≥50% within seven days, or urine output <0.5 mL/kg/hour for >6 hours.

Urgent assessment for significant/rising potassium, oliguria/anuria, fluid overload, systemic illness, severe renal deterioration or suspected infected obstruction. Review nephrotoxins and volume status, arrange appropriate repeat tests/ECG/imaging and specialist discussion. Do not tell everyone to drink large volumes regardless of HF/overload. A blocked tract may need urgent decompression; do not delay by arranging slow routine ultrasound.
## CKD diagnosis and explanation
CKD requires sustained abnormality for >3 months; G1/G2 require markers of kidney damage. Confirm chronicity and exclude AKI before labelling a single low eGFR.

| G category | eGFR mL/min/1.73 m² |
|---|---|
| G1 | ≥90 with damage marker |
| G2 | 60–89 with damage marker |
| G3a / G3b | 45–59 / 30–44 |
| G4 / G5 | 15–29 / <15 |
ACR: A1 <3, A2 3–30, A3 >30 mg/mmol. Explain both filtration and protein leakage; a lower number does not automatically mean dialysis is imminent. Discuss progression and CVD risk using individual data, not inevitability.
## CKD care and referral
Manage cause, BP, diabetes, smoking and cardiovascular prevention; consider ACE inhibitor/ARB for appropriate albuminuric disease with renal/potassium monitoring and suitable SGLT2 therapy under current eligibility. Statin is generally indicated; antiplatelet is not automatic primary prevention. Review renal dosing, avoid unnecessary NSAIDs and explain an individual illness/monitoring plan.

Monitor eGFR/ACR and complications according to risk. Refer for KFRE five-year replacement-therapy risk >5%, ACR ≥70 unless appropriately treated diabetic cause, ACR >30 with haematuria, sustained rapid decline, resistant hypertension or suspected inherited/renovascular disease. Suspected AKI or complications may need faster action than these chronic thresholds. [NICE NG203](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng203/chapter/Recommendations)
## Dialysis and transplant discussion
Clarify what the patient has been told, fears and effect on work/home. Haemodialysis filters blood externally; peritoneal dialysis uses the abdominal lining. Choice depends on clinical suitability, support and preference. Transplant assessment considers benefits, surgery, lifelong immunosuppression and monitoring. Conservative kidney management is also an active care option for selected people. Do not promise eligibility or present a fixed dialysis timetable for everyone.
## Suspected UTI
Ask dysuria, frequency/urgency, suprapubic pain, new nocturia, blood, vaginal/STI symptoms, fever, loin pain, pregnancy and previous cultures. Assess resistance risks, anatomy, catheters and renal function. Confusion or cloudy/smelly urine alone does not prove UTI.

For appropriate women <65 use the symptom-based UKHSA tool; dipstick is selective. **Do not routinely use dipstick to diagnose UTI in older adults or catheterised people.** Send culture when indicated, especially pregnancy, men, pyelonephritis/sepsis, recurrent/persistent infection or resistance/complicated disease. Asymptomatic bacteriuria is not ordinarily treated, with exceptions such as pregnancy and selected procedures. [UKHSA tool](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/urinary-tract-infection-diagnosis/diagnosis-of-urinary-tract-infections-quick-reference-tools-for-primary-care)

Uncomplicated lower UTI in a non-pregnant woman: suitable nitrofurantoin or trimethoprim according to renal function/resistance, usually three days; men generally seven days with culture. Pregnancy needs its own regimen. Nitrofurantoin does not treat pyelonephritis and is unsuitable when prostate involvement is suspected. Give symptom relief, hydration advice and review/escalation if worsening or no improvement within 48 hours.
## Recurrent UTI and pyelonephritis
Recurrent = ≥2 episodes/six months or ≥3/year. Confirm prior cultures and consider underlying cause. Discuss triggers, vaginal oestrogen where appropriate, selected prophylaxis or methenamine under current guidance; do not repeatedly prescribe without reassessment. [NICE NG112](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng112/chapter/recommendations)

Pyelonephritis: fever/rigors, loin pain, vomiting ± urinary symptoms. Send culture and assess sepsis, hydration, pregnancy and obstruction. Hospital assessment for systemic compromise, inability to take oral treatment, pregnancy/high-risk context or suspected obstruction; stable selected adults can follow an oral-treatment pathway with close safety-netting. Use renal-penetrating antibiotics and susceptibility/local guidance.
## Proteinuria, glomerular disease and PKD
Nephrotic pattern: heavy proteinuria, hypoalbuminaemia and oedema; assess thrombosis/infection risk and renal function, with prompt renal input. Nephritic pattern: haematuria with renal dysfunction, hypertension/proteinuria; rapid progression or systemic disease needs urgent specialist assessment. Biopsy/immune testing and treatment are specialist decisions.

ADPKD is inherited cystic kidney disease with possible hypertension, infection/bleeding and progressive dysfunction. Explain family/genetic implications and specialist follow-up; associated intracranial aneurysms are vascular abnormalities, not “brain cysts.” Acute severe headache needs emergency assessment.
## Stones and haematuria
Acute colic: assess infection, solitary kidney, renal impairment, pregnancy, uncontrolled pain/vomiting and alternative diagnoses. An infected obstructed system is an emergency. Suitable NSAID is first-line analgesia; urgent imaging/urology pathway as indicated, with pregnancy-specific imaging. Intervention depends on size/site/complications; selected distal ureteric stones may use an alpha-blocker.

After stones, encourage an individual fluid/intake prevention plan, normal appropriate dietary calcium and reduced excess salt; metabolic/stone analysis can guide further advice. Visible or persistent unexplained haematuria needs cause/cancer assessment even on anticoagulants or with a previous stone history. See [[Mens health]] for LUTS/prostate assessment.
## Example consultation phrases
- **Personal questions:** “With urinary symptoms, there are a few personal questions I need to ask to work out the cause and the right treatment.”
- **Clarify dysuria:** “Could you describe what you mean by burning when you pass urine?”
- **Associated kidney symptoms:** “Could I ask about a few symptoms that sometimes go with kidney problems?” Follow with specific symptoms relevant to AKI/CKD.
- **CKD:** “The kidneys aren't filtering as well as they should, or there are signs of lasting kidney damage. The results help us work out how much it is affecting you.”
- **Why act:** “There are two main aims: slowing any further kidney damage and reducing the risk of heart and circulation problems.”
- **Haemodialysis:** “Blood passes through a machine that removes waste and extra fluid, then returns the blood to your body.” Explain the individual schedule and whether home/centre treatment is being considered.
- **Peritoneal dialysis:** “Fluid is put into the abdomen through a tube. The lining inside acts as a filter, and the fluid is drained out after it has collected waste.”
- **Transplant expectations:** “A transplant can be a good option for some people, but it needs careful assessment. Medicines to prevent rejection and long-term monitoring are still needed.”
- **Glomerulonephritis:** “The kidney's tiny filtering units have become inflamed. There are different causes, so further tests may be needed.”
- **PKD:** “Cysts have formed in the kidneys. We monitor kidney function and treat problems such as blood pressure or pain; the kidney team will advise on your individual care.”
