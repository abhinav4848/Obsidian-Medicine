## LUTS — assess the pattern
Separate storage (frequency, urgency, nocturia), voiding (weak/intermittent stream, straining, hesitancy) and post-micturition symptoms. Ask onset, impact, retention, infection, haematuria, neurological symptoms and medicines/fluid/caffeine. Assess sleep, work, access to toilets, sexual effects and cancer concerns.

Examine abdomen/bladder, external genitalia and prostate where indicated, with consent/chaperone. Urinalysis and a frequency-volume chart/IPSS help assess cause and burden. Renal testing is indicated when impairment is suspected, not automatically for every uncomplicated LUTS.

**Urgent:** painful retention, sepsis, renal compromise, neurological bladder dysfunction or concerning cancer features. Do not label every male urinary symptom BPH.

## BPH and medicines
Explain benign prostate enlargement can affect urine flow but is not prostate cancer. Tailor caffeine/alcohol/fluid timing, bladder habits and double voiding without encouraging dehydration.

Moderate/severe bothersome LUTS can merit an alpha-blocker; discuss postural dizziness, falls and sexual effects, and review in 4–6 weeks. A 5-alpha-reductase inhibitor suits selected enlarged prostates/high progression risk (estimated >30 g or PSA >1.4 ng/mL), with slower benefit and later review; combination is appropriate in selected cases. Finasteride changes PSA interpretation and can cause sexual/mood effects. Persistent troublesome symptoms or complications need urology, with procedural choice individualised. [NICE CG97](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg97/chapter/Recommendations)

Counsel on depression/suicidal thoughts and persistent sexual dysfunction associated with finasteride; give current patient safety information and prompt review advice. [MHRA](https://www.gov.uk/drug-safety-update/finasteride-and-dutasteride-updated-safety-warnings-for-psychiatric-side-effects-and-sexual-dysfunction)

## PSA request and prostate cancer
Ask what triggered the request, understanding of the test, family history, ethnicity, symptoms and preferences. Explain PSA is **not a definitive cancer test**: benign/inflammatory causes can raise it, normal values do not exclude cancer, and testing may lead to MRI/biopsy, overdiagnosis or treatment harm.

Exclude UTI and consider recent infection/instrumentation, ejaculation/exercise and medicines before interpretation. Use current age-based/local thresholds and clinical context; a malignant-feeling prostate warrants urgent referral regardless of PSA. Do not delay an indicated referral while waiting for a repeat test. Suspected metastatic disease/cord compression needs urgent assessment.

For established cancer, clarify stage, surveillance versus active treatment, side effects and goals. Explain Gleason grading and MRI/biopsy only to the depth needed. Surgery, radiotherapy and hormonal/systemic therapy depend on specialist assessment; discuss sexual, urinary, bone, mood and family concerns.

## Prostatitis
Acute pelvic/perineal pain, urinary symptoms, fever or tender prostate: assess sepsis/retention and send urine culture. Severe illness or complications → hospital. If outpatient treatment is suitable, use current antibiotic guidance with review within 48 hours if not improving and reassessment after the initial course. Fluoroquinolones have significant safety restrictions; do not present them as risk-free defaults. Chronic pelvic pain/prostatitis needs a broader plan and urology input rather than endless empirical antibiotics.

## Erectile dysfunction
Clarify erection initiation, maintenance, libido and ejaculation rather than conflating them. Ask onset/situations, morning/solo erections, relationship, psychological factors, medicines and vascular/endocrine disease. Preserved spontaneous erections suggest a psychological contribution but do not prove absence of physical disease.

Assess CVD/metabolic risk and morning testosterone as appropriate, with relevant examination. Explore embarrassment and partner involvement with consent. Address causes, lifestyle and psychosexual support. PDE5 therapy such as sildenafil requires sexual stimulation; discuss timing, adverse effects and safety. **Never combine with nitrates or recreational nitrites/poppers.** Cardiovascular stability, interactions and renal/hepatic function affect suitability/dose. Painful prolonged erection or acute visual symptoms needs urgent help.

## Scrotal pain, swelling and lumps
Ask onset, pain, trauma, urinary/STI symptoms, systemic illness, undescended testis and change in shape/texture. Examination distinguishes testicular from extratesticular disease; explain ultrasound and uncertainty.

| Finding | Action |
|---|---|
| Sudden severe pain ± vomiting/high-riding testis | Suspected torsion: immediate surgical assessment; do not await ultrasound or rely on pain relief with elevation |
| Pain/fever/dysuria or discharge | Epididymo-orchitis possible; exclude torsion, test urine/STIs, treat under appropriate pathway |
| Painless testicular enlargement or texture/shape change | Urgent suspected-cancer assessment |
| New painful/non-draining or atypical varicocele | Assess promptly for underlying obstruction; appropriate urology/imaging |
| Hydrocele/epididymal cyst | Confirm diagnosis and exclude hidden testicular disease; treatment depends on symptoms |

Discuss fertility and support with suspected malignancy without assuming cancer from every lump. Undescended testis needs timely paediatric assessment; bilateral non-palpable testes need urgent evaluation. Do not wait indefinitely for spontaneous descent.

## Penile conditions and haematospermia
Phimosis, balanitis and Peyronie disease need history/examination, irritant/infective/skin assessment and sexual/urinary impact. Never force a foreskin back. **Paraphimosis** is an emergency. Recurrent balanitis may warrant diabetes/STI assessment. Persistent suspicious ulcer/mass after appropriate infection assessment → urgent penile-cancer referral.

Blood in semen is often benign but assess recurrence, age/risk, haematuria, pain and systemic features; persistent/unexplained symptoms or abnormal findings warrant urology assessment.

## Vasectomy counselling
Explore informed independent choice, future fertility, relationships and pressure. Explain intended permanence, alternatives, small failure risk, bleeding/infection and chronic scrotal pain. It is not immediately effective: continue contraception until the service confirms clearance after semen testing. Reversal is uncertain and should not underpin the decision. Discuss what the procedure/recovery involves and referral logistics.
