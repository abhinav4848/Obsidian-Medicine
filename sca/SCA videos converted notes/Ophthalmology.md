## Red, painful or visually impaired eye
Ask early: **pain? change in vision?** Then onset, laterality, photophobia, discharge/itch, contact lenses, trauma/chemical/high-speed exposure, flashes/floaters, headache/nausea and neurological symptoms. Review previous eye disease/surgery, medicines and inflammatory disease. Check visual acuity in each eye, pupils, movements and appropriate anterior/fundal examination when feasible; do not assume a remote image excludes serious disease.

**Same-day/emergency eye assessment:** significant pain, photophobia, reduced vision, corneal abnormality, contact-lens-related painful red eye, abnormal pupil, trauma/penetration, chemical injury, orbital signs or serious infection. Sudden visual loss needs immediate assessment even if painless. Arrange safe transport and no driving with unsafe vision.

## Conjunctivitis and surface disease
- **Conjunctivitis:** grittiness/irritation, redness and discharge; significant pain, photophobia or persistent visual reduction suggests another diagnosis. Viral disease usually settles with hygiene/symptom care. Bacterial disease often settles too; selected topical antibiotic/delayed strategy depends on severity and local guidance. Neonates and possible gonococcal infection need urgent specialist care. Allergic disease is often bilateral itchy/watery with atopy; suitable antihistamine/mast-cell therapy and trigger measures may help.
- **Subconjunctival haemorrhage:** sharply red patch without pain/vision change; check trauma, recurrence, BP and bleeding/anticoagulant context. Explain gradual clearance; atypical symptoms require reassessment.
- **Blepharitis/dry eye:** explain chronic tendency and consistent lid hygiene, warm compress/massage and suitable lubrication. Infection treatment is selective; persistent/atypical disease needs review.

## Eyelid swellings and malposition
Stye is usually acute tender infection; chalazion is a meibomian blockage/granuloma. Warm compresses and gentle care may help; persistent, recurrent, atypical or vision-affecting lesions need eye review. Do not squeeze. Entropion can abrade the cornea; ectropion causes exposure/watering. Lubrication and appropriate referral; pain/vision changes increase urgency.

## Glaucoma, uveitis and orbital cellulitis
**Acute angle closure:** severe painful red eye, blurred/halo vision, headache/nausea and abnormal pupil → emergency ophthalmology. Do not delay for routine pressure measurement. Chronic glaucoma can be asymptomatic; specialist assessment establishes optic-nerve/field damage and treatment. Raised pressure alone and glaucoma are not identical; glaucoma can occur at normal pressure.

**Uveitis/scleritis/keratitis:** pain, photophobia or visual change requires urgent eye assessment; associated IBD/rheumatological disease may guide investigation. Do not start steroid eye drops without appropriate specialist assessment.

**Orbital cellulitis:** swelling with painful/restricted movements, proptosis, visual change or systemic illness → emergency hospital assessment. Preseptal disease also needs careful assessment to exclude orbital involvement.

## Sudden visual loss, flashes and floaters
| Presentation | Action |
|---|---|
| Sudden painless loss, possible retinal artery occlusion | Immediate emergency eye/stroke pathway; do not delay for home manoeuvres |
| Retinal vein occlusion | Urgent ophthalmology; assess vascular risks and specialist treatment |
| New flashes/floaters, especially curtain/shadow or field loss | Urgent retinal assessment; same-day for vision/field change or high concern |
| Vitreous haemorrhage | Same-day eye assessment and search for underlying retinal disease |
| Transient monocular loss | Consider amaurosis fugax/TIA and urgent vascular assessment |
| Painful eye movement with visual change | Consider optic neuritis and urgent specialist assessment |

Absence of a dramatic fundal finding in primary care does not exclude retinal disease.

## Trauma and corneal infection
Chemical exposure: **start copious irrigation immediately** and arrange emergency assessment; do not postpone irrigation to finish history. Suspected penetrating injury/intraocular foreign body: protect without pressure, no attempted removal and emergency eye care. Contact lens wearers with pain/redness need urgent keratitis assessment. Herpes-related dendritic disease needs same-day ophthalmology and appropriate antiviral care; avoid unsupervised steroids/topical anaesthetic take-home treatment.

## Gradual visual loss
Assess course, glare, reading/driving, central versus peripheral change, falls, home safety, ADLs, mood and support. Optometry/ophthalmology assessment should identify the cause; diabetes/BP may need review.

- **Cataract:** lens clouding; surgery decisions depend on visual function and preferences, not a rigid acuity threshold. Childhood cataract requires urgent specialist care. Explain procedure benefits/risks and postoperative warning symptoms without guaranteeing perfect vision.
- **AMD:** central distortion/reading difficulty, drusen and risk factors such as smoking. Suspected active wet AMD requires urgent macula referral, normally within one working day. OCT guides diagnosis; anti-VEGF and low-vision support depend on findings. [NICE NG82](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng82/chapter/Recommendations)
- **Retinopathy:** attend risk-based diabetic screening and optimise diabetes/BP. New symptoms still need assessment between screens. Explain monitoring, laser/anti-VEGF or surgery according to specialist findings rather than a simple universal stage/treatment rule.

## Squint and amblyopia
Ask onset, constancy, diplopia, visual behaviour, headache/neurology and development. Acute/new squint with neurological symptoms or abnormal red reflex needs urgent assessment. Refer childhood squint for visual assessment to prevent amblyopia; glasses, patching/penalisation or surgery are specialist choices. Do not start patching the better eye without a plan.
