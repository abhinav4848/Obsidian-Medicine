## Gynaecological consultation essentials
Clarify symptom and agenda. Establish LMP/cycle, bleeding pattern, pain, discharge, pregnancy possibility, contraception and STI risk; these three considerations often coexist. Ask obstetric/surgical history, medicines, relevant cancer/VTE risks and screening history. Explain intimate questions/examination and offer a chaperone. Explore work, relationships, mood and fertility goals.

Acute pelvic pain with possible pregnancy, significant bleeding/instability, torsion, severe infection or new neurological bladder symptoms needs urgent assessment. Normal screening does not exclude symptomatic cancer.
## Contraception — choose with the patient
Ask purpose (pregnancy prevention, bleeding/acne/endometriosis), previous methods, pregnancy plans, adherence preferences, bleeding priorities, medicines, smoking, migraine/aura, BP/BMI and relevant disease. Use current **UKMEC** for the specific method: 1 unrestricted; 2 benefits generally outweigh risks; 3 risks usually outweigh benefits; 4 unacceptable risk. Avoid turning every caution into a blanket contraindication.

Discuss effectiveness in usual use, reversibility, side effects, STI protection, insertion/removal and access. Condoms add STI protection. Check coercion/safeguarding and whether the person can use the chosen method confidentially/safely.
## Combined hormonal contraception
Combined pill/patch/ring inhibit ovulation; tailored regimens may reduce hormone-free intervals where suitable. Assess VTE/arterial risk, migraine with aura, hypertension, smoking/age, postpartum/breastfeeding status and interacting medicines. Explain benefits and possible bleeding, nausea, breast/mood effects plus material clot risk; give specific urgent clot/neurological warning symptoms.

Starting and missed-pill rules depend on product/regimen. For a standard monophasic ethinylestradiol pill, one missed active pill usually does not require extra precautions; ≥2 missed pills can require seven consecutive active days of additional precautions. Timing in the pack, preceding hormone-free interval and UPSI determine EC need; misses near the end may require omitting the break. **Identify the actual brand and timing** before applying a rule. Vomiting/diarrhoea and enzyme inducers can also affect cover. Review BP, suitability and adherence; plan temporary alternatives around relevant major surgery/immobility.
## Progestogen-only pills
Traditional POP mainly alters mucus; desogestrel/drospirenone suppress ovulation more consistently. Explain daily use and expected irregular bleeding. Different windows and restart rules matter:

| POP | Late/missed threshold | Additional precautions after a missed active pill |
|---|---|---|
| Traditional norethisterone/levonorgestrel | >3 hours late | Usually 48 hours after correct use resumes |
| Desogestrel | >12 hours late | Usually 48 hours |
| Drospirenone 24 active/4 placebo | ≥24 hours late | Product/regimen-specific algorithm, commonly seven days; timing/number and placebo interval affect action |
Check EC indication using UPSI and preceding use; not every missed pill has the same consequence. Drospirenone is not managed by simply copying desogestrel rules. [CoSRH POP guidance](https://www.cosrh.org/Common/Uploaded%20files/documents/fsrh-ceu-clinical-guideline-progestogen-only-pills-aug22-amended-11july-2023-.pdf)
## Long-acting contraception and sterilisation
- **Copper IUD:** no hormones, highly effective, immediate contraception after suitable insertion; may increase bleeding/pain. Duration depends on device and age at insertion.
- **LNG-IUD/IUS:** thickens mucus and suppresses endometrium; often reduces bleeding. A 52 mg device can provide eight years' contraception in appropriate users; lower-dose devices differ. **HRT endometrial protection uses a five-year schedule**, not the contraceptive duration. [CoSRH IUC guidance](https://www.cosrh.org/Common/Uploaded%20files/documents/fsrh-clinical-guideline-intrauterine-contraception-mar-23-amended.pdf)
- **Implant:** effective reversible progestogen method, UK routine duration three years; discuss irregular bleeding, palpability, interactions and removal access.
- **Injection:** DMPA usually every 13 weeks; discuss bleeding/weight, bone-health context and delayed return of fertility. Review ongoing suitability rather than automatically stopping at two years.
- **Sterilisation:** intended permanent, small failure and procedural risks; discuss alternatives including LARC/vasectomy. Do not imply reversal is reliable or that obsolete procedures are routinely offered.

For insertion, reasonably exclude pregnancy and assess infection/bleeding concerns. Explain discomfort, expulsion/perforation risk where relevant and post-procedure warning signs. Do not remove an IUD without considering recent UPSI and replacement protection.
## Emergency contraception
Ask **all UPSI episodes this cycle**, timing, LMP/shortest cycles, regular contraception errors, earlier pregnancy risk, medicines/enzyme inducers, weight/BMI, breastfeeding and severe oral-steroid-treated asthma. Consider coercion, STI/PEP and ongoing contraception without delaying EC unnecessarily.

| Option | Key counselling |
|---|---|
| Copper IUD | Most effective; can be fitted within five days of first UPSI in cycle or within five days of earliest estimated ovulation when criteria met; provides ongoing cover |
| Ulipristal 30 mg | Up to 120 hours; delay hormonal contraception for five days, then use method-specific precautions |
| Levonorgestrel 1.5 mg | Licensed within 72 hours; can quick-start hormonal contraception, with method-specific precautions |
Oral EC is not reliable after ovulation and does not protect later intercourse. Recent progestogen, enzyme induction or weight can change choice/dose; Cu-IUD is often preferable in these situations. If fitting is delayed, consider appropriate oral EC meanwhile. Advise pregnancy test **21 days after the latest UPSI**, earlier review for concerning pain/bleeding, and product-specific vomiting/re-dose advice. Breastfeeding advice must follow the current method guidance. [CoSRH EC guideline](https://www.fsrh.org/Common/Uploaded%20files/documents/fsrh-guideline-emergency-contraception03dec2020-amendedjuly2023-11jul.pdf)
## Period changes and heavy bleeding
Define frequency, regularity, duration, flooding/clots, products used, pain, intermenstrual/postcoital bleeding and effect on life. Exclude pregnancy and assess anaemia/instability. Ask medicines (including anticoagulants), endocrine symptoms, bleeding history and previous fibroids/endometriosis.

HMB: FBC and cause-directed examination/investigation; thyroid/coagulation tests only when indicated. Hysteroscopy versus ultrasound depends on symptoms/risk. Suitable LNG-IUS is a common first option where no significant cavity-distorting pathology; alternatives include tranexamic acid, NSAID or suitable hormonal treatment. Large fibroids, persistent symptoms or endometrial concerns need gynaecology. Agree review and contraception/fertility implications. A short period-delay prescription also requires pregnancy and thrombotic-risk assessment.

Irregular/absent periods: consider pregnancy, contraception, PCOS, thyroid/prolactin disease, perimenopause, weight/exercise/stress and ovarian insufficiency. Headache/visual symptoms with galactorrhoea needs targeted assessment. Do not assume every irregular cycle is PCOS.
## Menopause and HRT
Usually diagnose clinically at ≥45 with typical symptoms and menstrual change; FSH has selected use at 40–45 or suspected POI <40, and hormonal contraception can affect interpretation. Menopause is 12 months without periods when not explained by hormones/other causes.

Explore vasomotor, sleep/mood, urogenital/sexual symptoms, work/function, beliefs and treatment goals. Options include HRT, menopause-focused CBT, local vaginal oestrogen and moisturisers/lubricants as appropriate. HRT is **not contraception**.

With uterus: systemic oestrogen needs adequate endometrial protection. After total hysterectomy: usually oestrogen only, with specialist exceptions. Choose route/regimen and explain individual breast, endometrial, VTE/arterial and bone effects; transdermal route can be preferable with increased VTE risk. Personal cancer or cardiovascular/VTE complexity needs expert advice, not automatic unsupported decisions. Review at three months then at least annually. Unexplained or persistent unscheduled bleeding needs assessment. [NICE NG23](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng23/chapter/Recommendations)
## Fertility concerns
Assess both partners where appropriate: duration/frequency of trying, ages, prior pregnancies with current/previous partners, cycles, pain/STIs, surgery, testicular history, medicines/anabolic steroids, lifestyle and sexual difficulty. Explore relationship/family pressure and emotional impact without assigning blame.

Offer preconception advice, folate and appropriate initial tests (ovulation assessment, semen analysis, relevant infection/endocrine tests). Referral is generally after one year of regular unprotected intercourse, earlier for age/risk or known cause. Explain local access/eligibility and uncertainty; do not jump immediately to IVF as the only option. Specialist care may include ovulation induction, surgery, IVF or ICSI according to cause.
## Incontinence
Separate stress, urgency, mixed, overflow and functional symptoms. Ask bladder/bowel habits, fluids/caffeine, infection/haematuria, prolapse, obstetric/surgical/neurological history and impact. Use diary and examination/urine testing appropriately; retention, pain, recurrent infection or neurological signs needs further assessment.

Stress/mixed: supervised pelvic-floor training for at least three months. Urgency/mixed: bladder training for at least six weeks, fluid/caffeine review and suitable medicines if needed. Antimuscarinic cognitive/constipation risk matters; beta3 agonists may suit selected patients. Duloxetine is not routine first-line stress-incontinence treatment. Explain escalation choices and referral rather than promising surgery.
## Cervical screening and abnormal results
Explain HPV primary screening identifies risk/pre-cancer, not an investigation for symptoms. Routine HPV-negative recall in England is generally five years, with exceptions based on prior history and programme rules. HPV positivity does not establish infidelity or mean cancer. Cytology and persistence determine surveillance/colposcopy; discuss the actual result letter.

Colposcopy is a closer examination with possible biopsy/treatment. Persistent postcoital bleeding, suspicious cervix or other concerning symptoms needs diagnostic assessment irrespective of a recent normal screen. [NHS programme](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/cervical-screening-laboratory-hpv-testing-and-cytology-services/cervical-screening-guidance-for-laboratories-providing-hpv-testing-and-cytology-services-in-the-nhs-cervical-screening-programme)
## Endometriosis, fibroids and PCOS
**Endometriosis/adenomyosis:** cyclical pelvic pain, dysmenorrhoea, deep dyspareunia, bowel/bladder symptoms or infertility. Arrange appropriate examination and ultrasound; a normal scan does not exclude endometriosis. Analgesia/hormonal treatment and referral depend on symptoms, preferences and fertility goals.

**Fibroids:** benign uterine muscle growths; may be incidental or cause bleeding, pressure, pain/fertility issues. Explain location/size and assess anaemia; medical, embolisation or surgical choices depend on goals and specialist findings.

**PCOS:** diagnose from an appropriate combination of ovulatory dysfunction, hyperandrogenism and polycystic morphology after excluding alternatives; an ultrasound alone is insufficient. Discuss menstrual/endometrial protection, metabolic risk, acne/hirsutism and fertility according to the patient's priority.
## Gynaecological cancer prompts
**Ovarian:** persistent/frequent bloating/distension, early satiety, pelvic/abdominal pain or urinary symptoms, especially new symptoms after 50. Ascites or suspicious pelvic/abdominal mass → suspected-cancer referral. In symptomatic people <40 do not use CA125 alone; consider urgent ultrasound. At ≥40, CA125 guides urgent ultrasound using current age thresholds:

| Age | CA125 threshold IU/mL |
|---|---|
| 40–49 | ≥35 |
| 50–59 | ≥31 |
| 60–69 | ≥24 |
| 70–79 | ≥25 |
| ≥80 | ≥31 |
Suspicious ultrasound → urgent cancer service. Below-threshold/normal imaging still needs alternative-cause assessment and symptom follow-up. [NICE NG12, 2026](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng12/chapter/recommendations-organised-by-site-of-cancer)

**Endometrial:** postmenopausal bleeding needs prompt assessment/appropriate urgent referral; HRT context does not automatically explain it. **Cervical:** suspicious cervix or concerning persistent bleeding → diagnostic pathway. **Vulval/vaginal:** unexplained lump, ulceration or bleeding needs appropriate urgent assessment. Explain suspicion, next steps and support without diagnosing cancer before confirmation.

## Example consultation phrases
- **Choose contraception:** “There are several ways to prevent pregnancy. Could I ask a few questions to work out which might suit you best?”
- **Establish the purpose:** “People use the pill for different reasons. Can I confirm what you want it for?”
- **Method preference:** “Do you have a particular pill or brand in mind? What do you already know about it?”
- **Embarrassment:** “I know periods can be difficult to talk about. These are concerns we discuss often, so please take your time and tell me what is troubling you.”
- **Period history:** “Can we go back to the beginning—when did your periods start, and how have they changed?”
- **Personal questions:** “As I mentioned, I need to ask a few personal questions. Are you sexually active? Can I ask about any pain with intercourse?”
- **More than pregnancy worry:** “I know you're worried about getting pregnant. Was there anything else worrying you?”
- **EC access:** “It's good that you've asked for help promptly. Emergency contraception is available through several services, including suitable pharmacies and sexual-health clinics.” Arrange the appropriate option without adding delay.
- **Menopause:** “The ovaries gradually stop releasing eggs and hormone levels change. The periods eventually stop, and those changes can cause symptoms such as hot flushes.”
- **Fertility assessment:** “We should assess both partners, because there are several possible reasons pregnancy hasn't happened.”
- **IVF:** “The egg is fertilised with sperm outside the body, then an embryo is placed into the womb.”
- **ICSI:** “A single sperm is injected directly into an egg in the laboratory.”
- **Pelvic-floor exercises:** “These strengthen the muscles supporting the bladder and help reduce leakage when you cough, laugh or strain.”
- **Cervical screening:** “The sample is first tested for high-risk HPV. If HPV is found, the same sample can be checked for cell changes.”
- **Colposcopy:** “It's a closer look at the cervix using a magnifying instrument. The team may use a dye and take a small sample if an area needs checking.”
- **Endometriosis:** “Tissue similar to the womb lining is growing outside the womb. It can cause pain and other symptoms depending on where it is.”
- **Fibroid location:** “Intramural means in the muscle wall, submucosal means growing towards the cavity, and subserosal means growing towards the outside.” Use only the terms relevant to their scan.
