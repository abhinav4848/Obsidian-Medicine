## Joint pain or swelling
Establish acute/chronic onset, pattern (one, few or many joints), symmetry, stiffness duration, activity/rest relationship, trauma and systemic symptoms. Ask skin/nails, eyes, bowel, infection, medicines and family history. Assess work, sleep, ADLs, mood and support; examine synovitis, range, neurovascular status and relevant extra-articular findings.

**Hot painful swollen joint:** consider septic arthritis even without fever, especially immunosuppressed/prosthetic joints. Suspected infection requires immediate same-day hospital assessment; do not give a steroid injection or label gout without considering it. Significant injury, neurovascular compromise or malignancy concern changes urgency.

## Osteoarthritis
Typical clinical diagnosis: age ≥45, activity-related pain and absent/≤30-minute morning stiffness, without atypical features. Imaging is not routinely needed. Explain a whole-joint condition rather than “inevitable wear and tear.”

Core care: tailored strengthening/aerobic exercise, weight support if appropriate, education and functional aids. Exercise may initially be uncomfortable but is beneficial when appropriately paced. Topical NSAID is first-line medicine for knee OA; consider suitable oral NSAID with gastroprotection if needed, accounting for GI/cardiorenal risk. Do not routinely offer paracetamol/weak opioids, strong opioids or glucosamine. Intra-articular steroid may provide short relief; explain limitations.

Refer for replacement assessment when symptoms substantially affect quality of life and non-surgical management is ineffective/unsuitable. Do not exclude purely on age or BMI. [NICE NG226](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng226/chapter/recommendations)

## Rheumatoid arthritis
Persistent synovitis, especially small joints, multiple joints or prolonged delay, needs urgent rheumatology referral **even with normal inflammatory markers or negative RF/anti-CCP**. Tests must not delay referral. Explain early disease-modifying treatment protects joints; analgesia alone does not prevent damage. [NICE NG100](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng100/chapter/Recommendations/)

For established RA, review disease activity, function, medicines/adherence and shared-care blood monitoring. Assess infection/toxicity, eye/chest symptoms, osteoporosis/CVD risk and support needs. Methotrexate is usually weekly; confirm schedule and folate plan. Short-term steroid bridging and DMARD/biologic changes require the appropriate specialist plan. New cervical/neurological symptoms need careful urgent consideration.

## Lupus and axial spondyloarthritis
**SLE:** photosensitive rash, oral ulcers, joint symptoms, Raynaud, systemic/renal/haematological features. Urine, renal function, FBC and targeted antibodies support assessment, not diagnosis from ANA alone. Organ-threatening features need urgent specialist care. Discuss sun protection, pregnancy planning, infection/medicine monitoring and rheumatology coordination.

**Axial spondyloarthritis:** inflammatory back-pain pattern (younger onset, prolonged symptoms, night/rest symptoms, improvement with movement), enthesitis, psoriasis/IBD/uveitis or family history. Refer appropriately; normal X-ray, inflammatory markers or negative HLA-B27 do not exclude it. Painful red photophobic eye → same-day ophthalmology. Exercise/physio and specialist disease treatment are central.

## Osteoporosis and fragility fracture
Usually asymptomatic until fracture. Assess prior fragility fracture, age, menopause, steroids, falls, BMI, smoking/alcohol and secondary causes. FRAX/QFracture and DXA refine fracture risk; urgent/high-risk treatment may be justified without waiting for a scan. Use risk-based NOGG thresholds rather than one percentage for every age.

DXA T-score ≤−2.5 supports osteoporosis; between −1 and −2.5 is low bone mass. Treatment decisions also depend on fracture history/risk, not the number alone. [NOGG](https://www.nogg.org.uk/full-guideline)

Oral bisphosphonate counselling: correct weekly/daily product, empty stomach, plain water, swallow whole, remain upright and delay food/other medicines for the product-specific interval (usually ≥30 minutes for alendronate). Review swallowing/oesophageal problems, renal function, calcium/vitamin D and dental issues. New swallowing pain, significant reflux, jaw symptoms or thigh/groin pain needs review. Reassess duration/risk rather than promising indefinite treatment without review; denosumab must not be stopped without an alternative plan because of rebound fracture risk.

Include falls prevention, adequate nutrition/calcium/vitamin D and appropriate exercise.

## Fibromyalgia
Chronic widespread pain with fatigue, sleep and cognitive symptoms can fit a fibromyalgia pattern after appropriate assessment. Validate symptoms, explain altered pain processing and assess overlapping disease/mood/function. Agree paced activity/exercise, sleep and psychological support; tailor medicines with regular benefit/harm review. Avoid escalating opioids or repeatedly ordering broad tests without a new indication. Diagnostic uncertainty or red flags needs reassessment rather than attributing everything to the label.
