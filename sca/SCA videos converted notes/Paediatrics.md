## Paediatric consultation foundations
Clarify who is present, parental responsibility, child's location and the child/young person's own voice. Tailor birth/neonatal, growth/development and immunisation history to the presentation. Ask feeding/intake, wet nappies/urine, activity, breathing, colour, fever and caregiver concern. A seriously unwell, floppy, apnoeic, cyanosed, poorly responsive or dehydrated infant needs urgent hospital care; do not be reassured by inability to describe symptoms.

Explore school/nursery, home changes, peers/bullying, parental wellbeing and support. Safeguarding belongs in clinical thinking throughout; it is not a scripted accusation in every case.

## Consent, confidentiality and young people
Assess understanding/maturity for the decision. Under 16, Gillick competence concerns sufficient understanding/intelligence for the proposed care; Fraser criteria specifically concern contraceptive advice/treatment, not every healthcare decision. Encourage safe parental/trusted-adult involvement but do not make it an automatic condition of confidential care.

Explain confidentiality and necessary exceptions. Safeguarding, coercion, exploitation and age differences need active consideration in sexual-health cases. Refusal and consent rules, especially under 18, are legally nuanced and differ across UK nations; seek senior/legal advice for serious disagreement rather than simply applying adult refusal rules.

## Behaviour change
Ask onset and specific examples, weekday/weekend pattern, sleep, physical symptoms, learning/hearing/vision, friendships, family changes, trauma and safety. Obtain the child's perspective and, with appropriate consent, school/health visitor information. Consider both neurodevelopmental and acquired causes; avoid diagnosing ADHD/autism from a single behaviour.

Agree practical support, school liaison and appropriate assessment/referral. Explain who will speak to the child, which services are involved and when review will occur. Preserve the child's dignity and avoid blaming parents or the child.

## Neonatal care, prematurity and screening
SGA describes size for gestation; growth restriction describes a pathological growth process and is not identical. LGA can reflect constitutional size or maternal diabetes. A premature baby has specific feeding, infection, respiratory and developmental follow-up needs; use corrected age appropriately.

Confirm newborn physical examination, hearing and blood-spot screening, results and follow-up. In England the routine blood-spot sample is usually day 5, with repeat/special schedules where needed; screening panels vary and can change. Explain screening identifies risk requiring confirmation, not a guaranteed diagnosis or proof of normal health.

## Neonatal jaundice
Ask age at onset in **hours**, gestation, feeding, nappies, weight, stool/urine colour, illness and risk factors. Do not estimate bilirubin severity from skin colour alone.

- First 24 hours: serum bilirubin within two hours and urgent medical review as soon as possible/within six hours.
- After 24 hours: visible jaundice still needs prompt bilirubin measurement, normally within six hours; interpret against age-in-hours and gestation-specific thresholds.
- Unwell baby, poor feeding, pale/chalky stool, dark urine or concerning progression needs urgent paediatric assessment.
- Prolonged jaundice (>14 days term, >21 days preterm) needs assessment for underlying disease, including conjugated jaundice.

Explain phototherapy and feeding support; breastfeeding can usually continue. “Breast-milk jaundice” requires a well baby and appropriate exclusion of pathology. [NICE CG98](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg98/chapter/recommendations)

## Faltering growth and development
Plot reliable serial weight/length/head measurements and compare trajectory, gestation and family pattern. Ask feeding technique, amounts, swallowing/vomiting, stools, illness, development and food access. Early excessive weight loss or failure to regain birth weight needs feeding/clinical assessment; do not attribute everything to constitutional size.

Observe feeds where useful, involve health visitor/dietitian and agree a monitoring plan. Development spans gross motor, fine motor, speech/language and social skills. Ask actual examples, hearing/vision and opportunity to practise; use current age-specific referral thresholds rather than treating course milestone examples as precise diagnostic cut-offs. **Loss of acquired skills** needs prompt specialist assessment.

## Immunisation discussion
Check the child's date of birth and actual record, because 2025–26 schedule transitions create different catch-up needs. As of the September 2026 UKHSA schedule:
- 8/12/16 weeks: six-in-one series; rotavirus at 8/12 weeks, MenB at 8/12 weeks and PCV at 16 weeks.
- Around one year: PCV, MenB and MMRV.
- Eligible 18-month cohort: additional six-in-one and MMRV.
- Preschool/adolescent boosters, HPV and annual childhood flu follow cohort/programme guidance.

Use the [current UKHSA schedule](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-complete-routine-immunisation-schedule/complete-routine-immunisation-schedule-from-1-july-2026) for exact birth-date/catch-up rules rather than assuming the same schedule for all children.

Explore the parent's particular worry or experience, explain benefits/common adverse effects and rare serious reactions proportionately, offer reliable information and keep discussion open. Refusing a vaccine alone does not automatically establish neglect.

## Childhood infection cases
Assess illness severity/hydration before labelling a rash. Ask immunisation, contacts, travel, pregnancy/immunosuppression exposure and nursery/school context. Suspected notifiable disease needs prompt health-protection advice; avoid bringing a possible measles case into a communal waiting room unannounced.

| Condition | Recognition and useful plan |
|---|---|
| Chickenpox | Vesicles in crops; fluids/comfort, avoid ibuprofen unless specifically advised; antivirals for selected risk groups. Exclude until all lesions crusted |
| Measles | Fever, cough/coryza/conjunctivitis then spreading rash; isolate, assess complications and notify urgently; exclusion usually four days after rash onset |
| Mumps | Parotid swelling ± systemic symptoms; supportive care, complications/notification advice; exclusion usually five days after swelling starts |
| Rubella | Mild rash/nodes; pregnancy-contact implications and notification; exclusion usually five days from rash onset |
| Scarlet fever | Sore throat, sandpaper rash/strawberry tongue; appropriate antibiotics and notification; exclusion at least 24 hours after antibiotics start |
| Slapped cheek | Parvovirus pattern; usually supportive; advise pregnant/immunocompromised contacts to seek advice |
| Hand, foot and mouth | Oral ulcers/hand-foot rash; hydration/analgesia; no routine exclusion if well enough |
| Pertussis | Prolonged paroxysmal cough ± whoop/vomiting; young infants may have apnoea. Assess urgency, antibiotics/contact protection and exclusion under public-health guidance |

Give specific worsening advice: breathing difficulty, reduced responsiveness, poor intake/urine or rapidly worsening illness. Do not present every viral infection as harmless merely because self-limiting is common.

## Limb pain and limp
Clarify pain/limp, injury, weight-bearing, fever, duration, night symptoms, swelling, developmental/hip history and safeguarding. Examine hip as well as knee and compare gait. **Unable to bear weight, severe pain, fever/systemic illness, acute neurological deficit or concerning injury → same-day acute assessment.**

Growing pains typically bilateral evening/night leg discomfort with normal daytime activity/examination; persistent focal pain, limp, swelling or systemic features contradict simple reassurance. Transient synovitis is considered after excluding septic arthritis and other important causes. Perthes usually affects a younger child; SUFE often an adolescent and can present as knee pain—suspected SUFE needs urgent assessment and no weight-bearing.

## ADHD and autism concerns
Assess persistent features across settings, developmental course, impairment, school reports, hearing/sleep, learning needs and mental health/safeguarding. ADHD concerns include attention, hyperactivity and impulsivity; autism assessment concerns social communication and restricted/repetitive patterns, with a wide range of strengths and presentations.

Refer through the appropriate specialist pathway; offer needs-based support while waiting. Medicines require specialist initiation/monitoring and do not replace educational/family support. Address parental expectations without promising a rapid diagnosis or “cure.”

## Bedwetting
Distinguish primary from secondary (previously dry ≥6 months), night-only from daytime symptoms. Ask stool/constipation, UTI/diabetes symptoms, fluids, sleep, changes/stress, toileting access and punishment. Assess recent onset, illness or neurological/urological red flags; urine testing is selective, especially new/daytime/systemic symptoms.

Reassure it is not deliberate. Encourage adequate daytime fluids, regular/pre-bed toileting and reward agreed behaviours, not dry nights. Treat constipation, support bedding/practical needs and agree whether an alarm suits the family. Desmopressin may suit rapid/short-term control or unsuitable alarm; explain relapse and **fluid restriction from one hour before to eight hours after** dosing, plus withholding/advice during vomiting/diarrhoea or illness affecting fluid balance. [NICE CG111](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg111/chapter/Recommendations)

## Safeguarding conversation
Concerning cues include injury inconsistent with development/history, unexplained bruising in a non-mobile child, delayed help, repeated punitive behaviour, sexualised behaviour or unmet basic needs. Risk factors are context, not proof of abuse.

Assess immediate safety, listen without leading interrogation, record exact relevant words/facts and consult safeguarding services promptly. Explain the concern and intended information-sharing when safe; parental refusal does not prevent necessary safeguarding action. Do not promise a particular social-services outcome or guarantee the child will remain at home. Arrange the actual referral/safe handover and a follow-up plan.
