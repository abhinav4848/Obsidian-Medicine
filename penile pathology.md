# Phimosis
Inability to retract foreskin- too tight
Usually normal up to age 6 - eventually settles itself. 

can lead to balanitis

Types:
- Physiological (primary): Gentle retraction over time +- steroids
- Secondary (e.g. due to recurrent balanitis, [[lichen planus]], [[eczema]], [[psoriasis]]): May need circumcision +- topical steroids
# Balanitis
Inflammation of glans penis
can be acute or chronic
### Causes:
- Skin conditions: e.g. irritant dermatitis, psoriasis
- Infection: Candida, [[chlamydia]]
- Trauma
- Malignancy
### C/f
- soreness, redness, dyspareunia, dysuria
### Inv
Swab, STI screen, **diabetes** screen
### Rx
Topical hydrocortisone, topical imidazole, antibiotics
### Complications
Phimosis, Urethral stricture, sexual dysfunction
# Peyronie's Disease
Abnormal curvature of penis due to asymmetrical fibrosis -> Painful erection and intercourse
**Rx**: Surgical correction (wait until 1 year of no disease progression)
Associated with [[upper limb ortho problems|Dupuytren's contracture]]- If someone with liver disease comes with this, may be worth asking about the penis as the two can be linked. 
# Penile cancer
- Penile mass or ulceration where STI has been excluded
- Persistent penile lesion after STI treatment
- Unexplained or persistent symptoms affecting foreskin or glans
