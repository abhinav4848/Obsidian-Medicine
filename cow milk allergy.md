Cow's milk protein intolerance/allergy (CMPI/CMPA) occurs in around 3-6% of all children 
- Typically presents in the first 3 months of life in ==formula-fed infants==, or with introduction of formula or solid foods. 
- Rarely it is seen in exclusively breastfed infants due to cow milk proteins in maternal diet. 
- Some babies react to a specific type of formula feed/solids only. 
# Clinical Features
(*Allergy focused history*)
- f/h/o food allergy/atopic disease (asthma, eczema, allergic rhinitis)
- Ask presenting symptoms, including:
    - Speed of onset of symptoms following food contact, and how much food was eaten.
    - Severity of reaction.
    - Duration of symptoms.
    - Frequency of occurrence, and reproducibility of symptoms on repeated exposure (if this has occurred).
- vomiting/regurgitation
    - milky vomits after feeds (ask if forceful)
    - may occur after being laid flat 
- Colic symptoms: excessive crying, especially while feeding
- diarrhoea 
- urticaria, atopic eczema
- wheeze, chronic cough
- rarely angioedema & [[als- anaphylaxis|anaphylaxis]] may occur

## Symptoms & Type of Allergy
Establish if it's CMPA based on Symptoms.
If it is allergy, see how quickly symptoms develop to establish IgE vs Non-IgE. (Significant overlap exists)
Mild/Moderate or Severe? Decide based on severity of symptoms. 
Look for persistence of these symptoms despite first-line measures as diagnosis can be wrong.

# Examination
- Weigh and measure the child, and plot on a centile chart to assess growth. 
- Look for any signs of malnutrition. 
- Look for signs indicating allergy-related comorbidities (atopic eczema, asthma and allergic rhinitis).
# Diagnosis
**Clinical** (e.g. improvement with cow's milk protein elimination). Investigations include:  
- skin prick/patch testing
- total IgE and specific IgE (RAST) for cow's milk protein
# Management
If the symptoms are severe (e.g. failure to thrive 🚩) refer to a paediatrician.  
### Management if formula-fed 
- extensive hydrolysed formula (**eHF**) milk is the first-line replacement formula for infants with mild-moderate symptoms
- Not tolerating **eHF** *or* **severe** CMPA (i.e. anaphylaxis, or faltering growth with non-IgE CMPA) -> amino acid-based formula (AAF)
- around 10% of infants are also intolerant to soya milk.
### Management if breastfed  
- continue breastfeeding
- eliminate cow's milk protein **from maternal diet**. 
- Give Calcium 1000mg & Vit D 10mcg for breastfeeding mothers whose babies have, or are suspected to have, CMPI, to prevent deficiency whilst they exclude dairy from their diet
- use eHF milk when breastfeeding stops, until 12 months of age and at least for 6 months
- If baby mixed fed, do both regimens. If baby not reacting to breastfeed, mum can have a normal diet even if baby was anaphylactic to non-breast milk dairy exposure. (RedWhale emphasize this). 

> [!Tip]- Can mum of baby with anaphylaxis to cow milk but tolerating dairy containing breast milk, continue to have dairy while breastfeeding? 
> #notAKT 
> *Can the mum of a baby who has had anaphylaxis to cows’ milk but always tolerated dairy-containing breast milk really continue to eat as much dairy as she likes while she breastfeeds?? Yes! Does that sound like a brave thing to recommend? Maybe?! But the MAP guidance specifically recommends we **don’t** to ask mum to exclude dairy from her diet in this scenario, and a friendly paediatric allergist filled us in on the two reasons why:*
> 
> - Firstly, the risk to baby of **anaphylaxis** to any allergen via breast milk is negligible (for example, we don't ask breastfeeding mums of babies who have had anaphylaxis to peanuts to stop eating peanuts). In this scenario where the baby is already not reacting to cows’ milk via breast milk, to go suddenly from not reacting **at all** to anaphylaxis is not a real clinical possibility. So, the risks are **negligible** but the potential harms are **real**, particularly the harm associated with mum getting fed up of her (not clinically necessary) dairy-free diet and stopping breastfeeding, thus losing the benefits for her and baby.
> - Secondly, there is likely to be a clinical **benefit** to the baby in micro-exposure to cows’ milk protein via breast milk in terms of the future prognosis of the allergy. In this setting where the allergy is particularly severe, that benefit is particularly important. 
> 
> *Remember that this **only** applies where the baby is fully tolerating breast milk (although this is the most likely scenario in mixed feeding) – if the baby is felt to be reacting to breast milk in any way, mum should exclude dairy from her diet. If you’re at all uncertain or uncomfortable, you could get advice from your local friendly paediatrician. Of course, we are fairly unlikely to find ourselves in this situation, as most of the time when we see babies with a history of anaphylaxis, they will already have had secondary care input during their trip to hospital, and hopefully the correct dietary advice as part of that.*

### How much to prescribe
Prescribe only 1 or 2 tins initially until compliance/tolerance is established, to avoid waste. The paediatric dietitian will advise as to recommended quantities as individual feeding requirements will vary, but as a guide (based on DH Bottle Feeding Guidelines):

| Age of child | Number of tins for 1m |
| ------------ | --------------------- |
| Under 6m     | 10 × 400g             |
| 6–12m        | 8× 400g               |
| Over 12m     | 6 × 400g tins         |
# Prognosis
CMPI usually resolves in most children  
- in children with IgE mediated intolerance, around 55% will be milk tolerant by the age of 5 years
- in children with non-IgE mediated intolerance most children will be milk tolerant by the age of 3 years
- a challenge is often performed in the hospital setting as anaphylaxis can occur.
