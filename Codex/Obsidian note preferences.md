# Obsidian note preferences
- Keep blank lines between prose paragraphs.
- Avoid unnecessary blank lines around headings, video/source lists, tables of contents, callouts and references; Obsidian supplies their visual spacing.
- Put one blank line before a table, and keep its header, separator and data rows together.
- Keep separate callouts distinct when tightening spacing; do not merge them into one blockquote.
- For SCA transcript notes, preserve the useful example consultation phrases and patient-facing explanations; do not reduce them to clinical summaries alone.
- Consolidate related videos by topic, remove promotional material, flag genuine uncertainties with discussion callouts, and use image-review callouts with the video name, timestamp and spoken cue. Do not include JSON filenames or links in the notes.
- Add `ai_created` to the YAML tags of AI-created notes and their indexes.
- Preserve full spoken consultation examples from the transcripts and user-edited wording. Do not replace them with shortened, generic or invented scripts. Keep any uncertainty separate from the quoted example.
- The reader is a doctor: retain clinical terminology and the original teaching emphasis (for example, safety-netting). Keep added commentary minimal and omit generic clinical reminders unless requested. Never present a paraphrase or an invented consultation script as a transcript quotation.
- Across all SCA video notes, standardise example patient placeholders such as `Missus X`, `Mrs X`, `Mister X` and `Mr X` to `Mr. X`, regardless of capitalisation, including within transcript-based consultation examples.
- Keep source timestamps only in video tables of contents and source-review callouts where the exact location is necessary (for example, missing images or unresolved wording). Omit timestamps and routine video/time citations beside consultation examples and teaching paragraphs unless specifically requested. Preserve clinical durations and follow-up intervals.
- Write teaching points directly. Avoid narration such as `the video mentions`, `the transcript contrasts`, or `the course teaches`; retain the useful content without describing its source. Use simple headings such as `Example wording`. Keep source-video lists, video TOCs and necessary source-review callouts.
- Preserve complete sets of useful questions from SCA transcripts. Present dense question lists as separate bullets under clear headings (for example, identity, geography, timing, transport and support), rather than compressing them into prose. Include the specific consultation sentence examples once in their relevant section; retain source wording and user-confirmed wording rather than replacing them with generic scripts.
- Use labelled bullets for consultation examples: **Purpose:** “Full sentence(s).” Keep source examples beside the teaching point they demonstrate, including what will happen at a planned appointment. Do not reduce them to prompts or fragments. Keep question sets as bullet lists, and do not invent a quoted script when the source only gives a checklist.
