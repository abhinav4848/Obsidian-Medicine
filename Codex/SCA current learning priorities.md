# SCA current learning priorities
## Context
Recorded on 19 October 2026 from feedback supplied after a mock consultation with a parent about their child's abdominal pain. The provider described the performance as a **bare pass (13/21)**. This is the provider's practice score, not an official RCGP SCA result. The observations below record that feedback; they have not been independently checked against a consultation transcript.

Assessment framework: [[sca/SCA mock exam framework]].

## Current weaknesses and practice goals
### 1. Make clinical reasoning clear — highest priority
- Summarise the important positive and negative findings before explaining the plan.
- Explain the working diagnosis, relevant alternatives and remaining uncertainty.
- If my assessment changes, explain which information changed it and why the new plan follows.
- Practice structure: “The features that make me think about X are… Y is less likely because… I cannot exclude Z, so I recommend…”

### 2. Do not assume examination findings
- Never describe an examination as normal when findings have not been provided.
- State what information is unavailable and how that limits the assessment.
- Explain how necessary examination or further assessment will be obtained.

### 3. Make referral plans practical
- Explain the destination, urgency, purpose and practical next steps.
- Explain that the receiving team will assess the patient and decide which investigations are appropriate.
- Avoid presenting a particular scan or procedure as routine or guaranteed without justification.
- Check whether the patient or carer can carry out the plan and address barriers.
- Specific feedback from this case: explain hospital examination and possible ultrasound; avoid suggesting CT as a routine next step.

### 4. Include safe interim symptom relief
- Consider what the patient can safely do while awaiting assessment.
- When recommending medication, check suitability and explain an appropriate dose or clearly identified dosing instructions.
- Specific feedback from this case: discuss appropriate paracetamol advice and check hydration and relevant asthma history before recommending ibuprofen.

### 5. Give specific safety-netting
- Explain which changes should prompt further help, how urgently and where to seek it.
- Tailor the advice to the working diagnosis, uncertainty and current plan.
- Specific feedback from this case highlighted severe or constant pain, fever, repeated vomiting and difficulty drinking as changes requiring further help.

### 6. Address follow-up and continuity
- Explain what should happen if symptoms persist after the planned assessment or treatment.
- Make responsibility and the route back to care clear.
- Specific feedback from this case: advise contacting the GP if symptoms continue after hospital assessment.

### 7. Involve the patient directly
- When consulting with a carer, include the patient where present and appropriate to their age, communication needs and capacity.
- Specific feedback from this case: ask the child a simple direct question about their pain.

### 8. Check understanding of the plan
- Ask the patient or carer to explain the agreed next steps in their own words.
- Check their understanding of urgency and safety-netting, and clarify any misunderstanding.
- Practice wording: “Just so I can check I've explained it clearly, could you talk me through what you'll do next?”

## Strengths to retain
The supplied feedback recognised targeted symptom history, attention to the effect on daily activities, recognition of a potentially serious cause, appropriate hospital escalation, acknowledgement of the parent's concern and agreement on a plan.
