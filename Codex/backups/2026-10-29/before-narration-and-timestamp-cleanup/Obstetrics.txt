## New pregnancy: first consultation
Confirm the positive test and **LMP/estimated gestation**. Ask whether the pregnancy is wanted and how the patient feels; do not assume happiness or distress. Check abdominal/pelvic pain, bleeding, severe vomiting or collapse before routine advice.

Review previous pregnancies, births, miscarriages, ectopic pregnancy, pre-eclampsia, gestational diabetes and fetal/neonatal problems. Review medical/mental health conditions, prescribed and non-prescribed medicines, alcohol/drugs, smoking, nutrition and relevant family history. Arrange prompt specialist medicine advice for potentially harmful treatment; avoid an unplanned cessation of essential therapy.

Explore partner/home support, domestic abuse in private, finances, housing, employment exposures and safeguarding. Agree the immediate priorities rather than delivering every antenatal fact at once.

**Plan:** refer/self-refer to maternity services for booking by **10 weeks**; arrange shared specialist care for significant disease or pregnancy risks. Standard uncomplicated schedules are around 10 appointments for a first pregnancy and 7 after a previous birth, with additional care when needed. Measure baseline BP, weight/BMI and arrange the usual booking assessment. [NICE NG201](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng201/chapter/recommendations).
## Supplements and healthy pregnancy advice
- **Folic acid:** 400 micrograms daily before conception and through the first 12 weeks. **5 mg daily** for increased congenital-malformation risk, including a personal/partner/family history or previous affected pregnancy, type 1/2 diabetes, relevant haematological disease, or medicines affecting folate. **Raised BMI alone does not require more than 400 micrograms.** Bariatric surgery needs individual specialist micronutrient advice. [NICE NG247](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng247/chapter/Recommendations).
- Discuss vitamin D supplementation, usually **10 micrograms daily**, particularly during autumn/winter and year-round where indicated by skin colour, limited sunlight or other risk.
- Avoid alcohol; offer smoking cessation support and pregnancy-appropriate treatment. Ask about recreational drugs without judgement.
- Discuss safe food preparation and avoidance of high-risk foods, liver/high-dose vitamin A supplements and excess caffeine. Encourage appropriate exercise and a balanced diet; tailor advice to nausea, work and existing illness.
## Screening, scans and rhesus status
Explain that screening is offered with informed choice. Booking tests include FBC, blood group/RhD and antibody testing, and screening for HIV, hepatitis B, syphilis and haemoglobinopathies as appropriate to the programme.

Discuss dating/combined screening and the later anomaly scan using the maternity programme's timing. **Screening estimates probability; it is not a diagnostic test.** A higher-chance result leads to discussion of further screening or diagnostic testing, including benefits, limitations and procedural risks. Ask what the result would mean to the patient rather than assuming a preferred decision.

For an RhD-negative patient, explain sensitisation and the role of anti-D. The maternity team determines prophylaxis around routine antenatal care, sensitising events and birth, taking antibody status and fetal information into account.
## Unplanned pregnancy and abortion request
Establish gestation, symptoms, wishes and decision certainty. Speak privately about pressure, coercion, abuse and safety. Support a voluntary decision; partner approval is not required. Offer non-directive discussion of continuing the pregnancy, adoption and abortion according to what the patient wants to consider.

Provide timely access to an abortion provider, including self-referral where available; counselling is optional. The patient can change their mind before treatment. Explain confidentiality and its safeguarding limits, particularly for younger patients. A conscientious objection must not obstruct access or urgent care.

Explain **medical** treatment with mifepristone/misoprostol versus a **procedure**, with choices determined by gestation, clinical circumstances, preference and service availability. Early treatment may be possible at home after assessment. The provider explains pain relief, expected bleeding, complications and how completion is confirmed. Discuss practical support, travel, time off and contraception without delaying access. [NHS abortion information](https://www.nhs.uk/tests-and-treatments/abortion/).

Safety-net severe/unilateral pain, shoulder-tip pain, collapse, heavy bleeding, fever or significant deterioration; do not assume every symptom is simply due to the pregnancy.
## Pain or bleeding in pregnancy
**Triage first:** gestation; severity and onset of pain; bleeding amount/clots; faintness/collapse; shoulder-tip pain; fever; vomiting; urinary symptoms. In later pregnancy, ask about contractions, fluid loss and **changed/reduced fetal movement**. Consider obstetric causes alongside appendicitis, biliary disease, urinary infection/stones and other surgical illness.

- **Haemodynamic compromise, severe pain, suspected rupture/abruption or sepsis:** emergency transfer.
- **Stable early pregnancy with pain/bleeding:** prompt early pregnancy/gynaecology assessment according to symptoms and risk. Atypical ectopic pregnancy is possible without recognised risk factors.
- **Later bleeding, significant pain or reduced movement:** contact maternity triage urgently; do not wait for the next routine appointment.

Do not use a home Doppler or a reassuring earlier scan to dismiss new significant symptoms.
## Ectopic pregnancy and miscarriage
**Ectopic clues:** pain, bleeding, shoulder-tip pain, dizziness/syncope, previous ectopic, tubal surgery/PID or assisted conception. Explain that a pregnancy outside the womb can bleed internally. Stable suspected ectopic needs urgent specialist assessment; instability or severe symptoms requires an ambulance. Assessment uses transvaginal ultrasound and, where needed, serial hCG; **hCG alone does not establish location**. Management may be expectant, medical or surgical according to specialist criteria and the patient's situation.

**Miscarriage:** acknowledge distress and avoid blame. Bleeding does not necessarily mean pregnancy loss. Establish viability/location with appropriate assessment; do not diagnose miscarriage from the cervical os or one uncertain early finding alone. Discuss expectant, medical and surgical options after diagnosis, with follow-up, analgesia and return instructions.

With **scan-confirmed intrauterine pregnancy, current bleeding and a previous miscarriage**, offer vaginal micronised progesterone **400 mg twice daily**; continue to **16 completed weeks** if fetal heartbeat is confirmed. [NICE NG126](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng126/chapter/management-of-miscarriage).

For ectopic pregnancy or miscarriage up to **11+6 weeks**, anti-D is not offered. Later events require the RhD/gestation/event-specific pathway. [NICE anti-D update](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng126/resources/resource-impact-statement-pdf-8779033659589). Offer bereavement support and follow-up; explore recurrent-loss referral when relevant.

> Before adding a dose rule for **12 weeks or later**, confirm the final NG126 event-specific recommendations, available preparation and your early pregnancy service’s pathway.
## Gestational diabetes
Risk factors include BMI **≥30**, previous baby **≥4.5 kg**, previous gestational diabetes, first-degree family history of diabetes or ethnicity with high diabetes prevalence. Offer **75 g OGTT at 24–28 weeks**; after previous gestational diabetes, offer early self-monitoring or early OGTT, repeating OGTT at 24–28 weeks if initially normal.

Diagnostic OGTT thresholds: fasting **≥5.6 mmol/L** or 2-hour **≥7.8 mmol/L**. Arrange joint diabetes–antenatal review within **1 week**, dietary support and self-monitoring. Explain effects on fetal growth, birth and neonatal glucose, and that treatment reduces risk.

Diet/activity may be tried when fasting glucose is below 7; add metformin if targets are unmet after 1–2 weeks, or insulin if unsuitable/insufficient. Fasting **≥7** warrants immediate insulin, with or without metformin; consider this earlier with macrosomia/hydramnios. Individualise with the team. Plan postnatal diabetes testing and annual surveillance. [NICE NG3](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng3/chapter/Recommendations).
## Hypertension and pre-eclampsia
New hypertension after 20 weeks is gestational hypertension if pre-eclampsia features are absent. **Pre-eclampsia can involve organ dysfunction without proteinuria.** Ask about severe/persistent headache, visual disturbance, epigastric/right-upper-quadrant pain, vomiting, sudden swelling, breathlessness, reduced urine and reduced fetal movement. It can also occur after birth.

A first BP **≥140/90 after 20 weeks** needs secondary-care assessment within **24 hours**; symptoms or severe readings require more urgent action. BP around **160/110 or above**, seizures or serious symptoms need emergency obstetric assessment. Do not wait for a second routine visit or a urine result when clinically concerning.

Secondary care assesses maternal blood/urine tests and fetal wellbeing, treats BP (often labetalol, with alternatives according to suitability), and considers magnesium sulfate and delivery when indicated. Explain stabilisation and monitoring; timing of delivery balances maternal and fetal risks.

**Prevention:** prescribed aspirin **75–150 mg daily from 12 weeks until birth** for one high-risk factor (previous hypertensive pregnancy, CKD, autoimmune disease, type 1/2 diabetes or chronic hypertension), or **more than one** moderate factor: first pregnancy, age ≥40, interval >10 years, booking BMI ≥35, family history or multiple pregnancy. [NICE NG133](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng133/chapter/Recommendations).
## SCA close
State the destination and urgency of any referral, how the patient will get there, who will support them and what to do if symptoms worsen before assessment. For routine care, check the patient knows how to access their maternity service and whom to contact out of hours.
## Example consultation phrases
- **Folic acid:** “Can we talk about folic acid? Since you haven't started it yet, let's arrange the right supplement for you.” (V68, 12:10)
- **Screening choice:** “Screening estimates the chance of the baby having the condition. It doesn't tell us for certain. We can discuss what the test can show and what you would want to do with that information.” (V68, 18:55)
- **Abortion options:** “There are medicines that end the pregnancy, and there are procedures. The provider can discuss which options are suitable at this stage and what each involves.” (V68, 30:42)
- **Conscientious objection, if relevant:** “I don't provide this treatment myself, but I will make sure you can speak promptly with a colleague or service that can help.” Do not obstruct access or delay urgent care. (V68, 29:52)
- **Recurrent loss:** “I can see why you'd want to understand whether there is a cause and whether anything can reduce the chance of this happening again. We can discuss specialist assessment and support.” (V69, 11:19)
- **Ectopic pregnancy:** “It means the pregnancy is developing outside the womb, most often in a fallopian tube. It can cause internal bleeding, so we need urgent assessment.” (V69, 12:06)
- **Gestational diabetes risk:** “Raised sugar in pregnancy can affect the baby's growth and increase the risk of complications. Treating it helps reduce those risks.” (V69, 15:14)
- **Pre-eclampsia and delivery:** “Medicines can control the blood pressure, but the team also needs to decide the safest timing of delivery. Monitoring continues after birth too.” (V69, 23:36)
