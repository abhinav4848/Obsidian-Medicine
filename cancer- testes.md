Age Breakdown for what a testicular lump could be: 20-30yo. 

#mnemonic TSML (L for later in life)

| Testicular lump diagnosis | Age       |    Peak age | Approx. proportion |
| ------------------------- | --------- | ----------: | -----------------: |
| Teratoma                  | **20–30** |   ~25 years |               ~30% |
| Seminoma                  | **30–40** |   ~35 years |               ~40% |
| Mixed teratoma–seminoma   | **25–35** | 25–35 years |               ~15% |
| Lymphoma                  | **60–70** | 60–70 years |                <7% |
# Types
Most Testicular cancers are: 
- Germ Cell tumours, which can be
	1. Seminomas
	2. Non-Seminomas (e.g. embryonal, yolk sac, teratoma and choriocarcinoma)
- Non-Germ Cell tumours
	1. Leydig cells
	2. Sarcomas

# Risk Factors 
- infertility (increases risk by a **factor** of 3)
- [[undescended testes|Cryptorchidism]] 
- [[cancer- risk factors|family history]] 
- [[childhood syndromes|Klinefelter syndrome]] 
- [[mumps]] orchitis 
# Clinical Features
- Painless lump, hard, irregular, not fluctuant, can't **transilluminate**
- Pain in minority
- 🚩[[scrotal problems|Hydrocele]]- can be the first sign of **cancer**. 
	- new hydroceles in males aged 20-40 years old must be investigated by way of urgent scrotal ultrasound.
- [[gynaecomastia]]
	- This is due to high E:A ration
	- germ-cell tumours → hCG → Leydig cell dysfunction → increase in both oestradiol and testosterone production, but rise in oestradiol is relatively greater than testosterone
	- Leydig cell tumours (non-seminoma) → directly secrete more oestradiol and convert additional androgen precursors to oestrogens
# Investigation
Do 2ww if non-painful enlargement, change in shape, change in texture. 
If not the above + unexplained/persistent testicular problems -> Consider USS. 
# Management 
- treatment depends on whether the tumour is a seminoma or a non-seminoma
- orchidectomy
- chemotherapy and radiotherapy may be given depending on staging and tumour type

Prognosis is generally excellent  
- 5 year survival for seminomas is around 95% if Stage I
- 5 year survival for teratomas is around 85% if Stage I
