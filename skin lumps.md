# Ganglion
A ganglion is subdermal (not attached to the skin), so ganglions don't move with skin. Skin should move over a ganglion. Ganglions transilluminate as they're fluid filled.

# Diff

| Feature                   | Lipoma                              | Ganglion cyst                                             | Neuroma                                                                                          |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Origin**                | Benign tumour of fat                | Fluid-filled cyst from a joint or tendon sheath           | Benign growth/thickening of nerve tissue                                                         |
| **Consistency**           | Soft, doughy                        | Firm to tense, fluctuant                                  | Firm                                                                                             |
| **Mobility**              | Very mobile under the skin          | Less mobile, attached to underlying joint/tendon          | Usually fixed along the course of a nerve                                                        |
| **Pain**                  | Usually painless                    | Usually painless, may ache with movement                  | Often painful or tender; may cause burning or electric-shock pain                                |
| **Neurological symptoms** | None                                | Rarely, unless compressing nearby structures              | Common- numbness, tingling, altered sensation                                                    |
| **Skin changes**          | Normal                              | Normal                                                    | Normal                                                                                           |
| **Transillumination**     | No                                  | Sometimes yes (especially superficial ganglia)            | No                                                                                               |
| **Common locations**      | Back, shoulders, neck, trunk, limbs | Dorsum of wrist (most common), volar wrist, fingers, foot | [[foot problems\|Morton's neuroma]] (between 3rd–4th toes), traumatic neuroma, peripheral nerves |
